Weed and Your Brain

THC borrows your brain's own system.

What's going on

Your brain has its own cannabis-like chemicals that help tune memory, mood, appetite, and sleep. THC plugs into the same spots, and it's much stronger. Edibles are slow to start and hard to stop.

"Basically candy"→"Nothing's happening"→A second gummy→Both kick in→No way to stop it

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "It's natural, so it's safe."
  • "It's no worse than candy."
  • "Nothing's happening. Another one."

🏠 Situations

  • gummies in a sibling's room
  • vape pens, no idea what's inside
  • a friend high a lot

🫀 Too high

  • shaking, heart pounding
  • "I think I'm dying"
  • can't stay awake

Where to step in

Know your why. Have your line.

  1. Know your why: what are you hoping it'll do? Then ask what else gets you that.
  2. Have your line ready: "Nah, I'm good." "I have a game tomorrow."
  3. Friend too high? Calm, quiet spot, water, slow breathing. Stay with them and get an adult.

If they have chest pain, can't stay awake, or something feels like an emergency, call 911 or get an adult to take them to the ER.

Part 1 · Understand it

What THC does to a growing brain

Sound familiar?Forget both extremes. Here's the science.
  • Kids talk about weed like it's no big deal, or like it's the worst thing ever.
  • Someone said it's natural, so it must be safe.
  • You've seen gummies or vape pens and weren't sure what was in them.
  • It's legal for adults where you live, and someone in your family uses it.
  • You're curious what it actually does.
  • You're worried about a friend who seems high a lot.

You've probably heard both extremes. The real science is more interesting than either one, and you can handle it.

How many students actually use itMost of your grade isn't using.
Where THC lands in your brainTHC borrows your brain's own system.
🍬 Edibles: the slow trapEdibles: slow to start, hard to stop.

The most common edible mistake: "It's not working," so they eat another one. Then both kick in.

The worst part: once it's eaten, there's no way to speed it up or stop it. You wait it out.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Making your own call

What actually helps

Know your why. Have your line.

Tap one to see how.

🧭 Know your why.

If you're deciding about weed, notice what you're hoping it'll do: relax, sleep, fit in, try something new. Then ask what else gets you that.

💬 Have your line ready.

"Nah, I'm good." "My parents would know." "I have a game tomorrow." Short is fine.

🚗 Never drive or ride high.

THC slows reaction time and messes with judging distance. Call a parent or another adult you trust, a friend who's sober, or a ride. Lots of families have a "pick me up, no questions tonight" deal. Ask for one.

🫂 Look after a friend who's too high.

Find a calm, quiet spot. Water, a snack, slow breathing. Remind them it will pass. Stay with them. If they have chest pain, can't stay awake, or something feels like an emergency, call 911 or get an adult to take them to the ER.

📉 Cutting back? Expect a rough week.

If someone you know is trying to stop, the first week or two can be irritable and restless. It gets better.

🧑‍🏫 Talk to someone.

Counselors and nurses help students with this all the time. If you're worried about what stays private, ask that first.

Tap an answer for each one.

Weed is natural, so it's harmless.

Today's weed is several times stronger than it was in the 1990s.

If an edible isn't working after 30 minutes, take more.

👥 In a groupOne sleepover. One bag of gummies.

👥 For advisory or class · about 10 minutes · no materials needed

  1. Read the story out loud. Sam is sleeping over at Alex's house.
  2. At each stop, everyone votes with fingers: 1, 2, or 3. No talking until the vote.
  3. Ask a couple of people why they picked what they picked. Then tap to see what usually happens.
👥 The gummies · 1 of 3Stop 1 · 10:30pm

Alex finds a bag of gummies in an older sister's room. "They're edibles. Basically candy with a little extra."

  1. Try one, it's just candy
  2. Say "I'm good" and grab actual candy
  3. Text a parent your code word to get picked up
What usually happens

2 and 3 both work. There's no way to tell how strong a gummy is by looking, and one can be several times what an adult would take.

👥 The gummies · 2 of 3Stop 2 · 11:30pm

Alex ate one an hour ago. "Nothing's happening. I'm having another."

  1. Say go for it
  2. Tell Alex to wait, it can take two hours
  3. Go to sleep and stay out of it
What usually happens

2. Edibles can take up to two hours to kick in. The second gummy is how most people end up sick.

👥 The gummies · 3 of 3Stop 3 · 12:45am

Both gummies hit at once. Alex is shaking, heart pounding, and says, "I think I'm dying."

  1. Leave Alex alone to sleep it off
  2. Stay calm with Alex and wake up an adult
  3. Look it up online first
What usually happens

2. Talk slowly, offer water, and get an adult. If Alex faints, has chest pain, or can't stay awake, call 911 or Poison Control. Getting help never makes it worse.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Which stop was the hardest? What made it hard?
  • Why do you think edibles look so much like regular candy?
  • What could Sam say at Stop 1 that wouldn't feel awkward?
Tip for the person leading

Keep it about Sam and Alex, not anyone in the room. Make Stop 3 clear: stay with them and wake an adult. Nobody gets in trouble for getting help.

Try itReal moments. What's your move?

Pick a move. There may be more than one good one.

Your friend says their cousin vapes weed and it's "no worse than candy."

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

Get help right away if

  • Someone has chest pain, or their heart won't slow down.
  • Someone can't stay awake or can't be woken up.
  • Someone keeps vomiting and can't stop.
  • Someone is seeing or hearing things, or is so paranoid they could hurt themselves or someone else.
  • A little kid ate an edible.

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room for an emergency. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222: free, private, with interpreters in many languages. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. One of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

Frog and Toad Together · Arnold Lobel, 1972 Throwback

In "Cookies," Frog and Toad can't stop eating. They try a box, a string, a high shelf, and finally give the cookies to the birds. Willpower works better with a plan, and that's true for gummies too.

🎬 Watch

Brave · Pixar, 2012

Merida gets a cake from a witch, hoping it will change her mom. It does, into a bear, and there's no quick way to undo it. Once it's eaten, you wait it out. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Brave · Sara Bareilles, 2013

"Say what you wanna say." A song about getting the words out. Have your line ready: "Nah, I'm good" is enough.

You've Got a Friend · Carole King, 1971

"You just call out my name." If a friend is too high, the lesson's job is simple: stay with them, keep it calm, and get an adult if it looks like an emergency.

🖼️ Look

Sunny Side Up · Jennifer L. Holm and Matthew Holm, 2015

Sunny figures out, a little at a time, that her big brother's problem is drugs and drinking. Her grandpa is the one who finally talks to her about it straight. A graphic novel.

Heads up: Sunny's older brother's drinking and drug use get worse, and the family fights about it. The ending is hopeful.
