Part 1 · Understand it

Why comparison hits so hard online

Sound familiar?You know it's edited. It still stings.
  • You scrolled for ten minutes and came away feeling worse about your life, your looks, or your friends.
  • You compared your follower count or likes to someone else's.
  • Everyone seems to have a perfect body, perfect friends, perfect weekends, or a perfect college list.
  • You deleted a post because it didn't get enough likes.
  • You edited a photo of yourself until it didn't really look like you.
  • You know it's fake, and it still gets to you.

Comparing yourself to others is a normal human thing. Social media turns it up to a volume that's hard to handle.

How the trap works

Tap each one.

🎞Highlight reels

People post their best moments, best angles, and best news. You see everyone else's best next to your everyday.

✨Filters and editing

Faces, bodies, and rooms are smoothed, slimmed, and brightened. Some of what you see isn't physically real.

🔢Numbers on everything

Likes, views, and followers turn social life into a scoreboard, and your brain notices scores.

Why it hits so hardYour brain compares. Feeds never stop.
Your brain is built to compare.
Humans have always sized themselves up against others. It used to be a handful of people. Now it's thousands, including professionals and celebrities.
Teen brains are tuned to social feedback.
Research has found that likes activate the brain's reward system, and teens are especially sensitive to what peers think.
It's endless.
There's always someone who seems prettier, richer, more popular, or more talented. The scroll never runs out of people to compare to.
It's not just social media.
Grades, sports, and who got invited where. Feeds amplify comparisons that already happen at school.
🪞 You're comparing the wrong thingsYour inside vs. their best outside.

You're putting your inside (your doubts, your bad days, your unedited face) next to everyone else's outside (their best day, their best angle, their best filter). That comparison is rigged before it starts.

The worst part: the people you're comparing yourself to are often comparing themselves to someone else, and feeling just as behind.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Your feed should work for you.

Tap one to see how.

🔍 Notice the after-feeling.

After scrolling, ask: do I feel better or worse? Your feelings are data about which accounts are good for you.

🧹 Curate your feed.

Mute or unfollow accounts that make you feel bad, even friends. Muting is private. Follow things that make you laugh, learn, or feel inspired.

🎬 Remember the editing.

Every time you see a perfect post, remind yourself: chosen, angled, edited. Not the whole story.

📏 Compare to past you.

Instead of measuring against others, notice how you've grown since last year.

🙈 Hide the numbers.

Many apps let you hide like counts. Try it for a week and see how it feels.

🌳 Do more, scroll less.

Time spent doing things you enjoy builds confidence. Time spent watching other people do things usually doesn't.

Try itWhich accounts leave you feeling worse?

Next time you scroll, notice how each account makes you feel. Check what fits for the accounts you see most.

🔇 Mute

Try itScroll moments. What's your move?

Pick the most helpful move.

An influencer you follow always makes you feel bad about your body.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese keep you stuck in the trap.
🔁 Scrolling to feel better.

Feeling bad and scrolling more rarely works. Put it down and do something else.

🎭 Performing your life.

Posting to look happy instead of being happy is exhausting.

🧮 Counting likes.

Likes measure timing, algorithms, and luck as much as anything about you.

🙊 Keeping it to yourself.

Talking about how feeds make you feel often reveals that friends feel the same way.

When to get more help

  • Comparing yourself is affecting how you eat, sleep, or feel about your body most of the time.
  • You feel worthless or hopeless after being online.
  • You're being targeted or bullied online.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.
