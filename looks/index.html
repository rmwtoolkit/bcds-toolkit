Four ways to package a lesson

Same lessons, new packaging. Each format takes something teens hold in their hands and turns it into the page. They are working pages: tap, slide, press play, and try the check-in. Nothing typed or tapped in them is saved or sent. Each has a BCDS version and a generic version with any school's name and colors.

The Zine

Made by hand

Paper grain, label-maker tags, a taped note, handwriting in the margins, and a check-in cut like a coupon.

Shown on: Under Construction. Fits personal, do-it-yourself lessons.

BCDSGeneric
The Record

An album you just bought

Slide the record out of the sleeve. The back-cover track list is the menu: Side A to understand it, Side B to try one. The liner notes are the lesson.

Shown on: Under Construction. Fits the big growing-up lessons.

BCDSGeneric
The Mixtape

A tape someone made you

Press play and the reels turn. The track list is handwritten on the card inside the case, with the same Side A and Side B and the same liner notes as the record.

Shown on: Under Construction. Fits friendship and relationship lessons.

BCDSGeneric
The Diner

A coping menu

The tools are menu items, priced by how long they take. Tap what you'd order and it lands on your guest check. The myths are 86'd, which is diner slang for off the menu.

Shown on: Anger Is Information. Fits coping and skills lessons. Never for body image or eating lessons.

BCDSGeneric

The same in every format

  • The check-in and Get help come in the same order and look familiar, so a student never has to hunt for 988.
  • The research, discussion activity and media picks fold into "For teachers and the curious" at the bottom.
  • The privacy promise sits up top: nothing saved, nothing sent.
  • About a third of the words of the current lessons.

If you're showing students

  1. Which one would you actually scroll to the end of?
  2. Which feels like it was made for you?
  3. Which one looks like AI made it?
  4. What would you cut?
