The Inner Critic

The meanest voice might be your own.

What's going on

Almost everyone has an inner critic. It thinks it's protecting you by getting to you first. Its methods are terrible, and it's not telling the truth about you.

You make a mistake→The critic gets there first→Bad stuff sticks more→Compliments slide off→You believe the critic

💭 Thoughts

  • "You're so stupid."
  • "Nobody will like you."
  • "Everyone's better than you."

🫀 Body

  • your face gets hot
  • a sinking feeling

🔁 Behaviors

  • replaying embarrassing moments
  • only seeing what's wrong
  • scrolling and comparing

Part 1 · Understand it

Where the inner critic comes from

Sound familiar?That mean voice in your head? Everyone has one.
  • A voice in your head says "you're so stupid" when you make a mistake.
  • You'd never talk to a friend the way you talk to yourself.
  • Someone gave you a compliment and you immediately thought of reasons it wasn't true.
  • You look in the mirror and only see what's wrong.
  • You replay embarrassing moments at night, over and over.
  • You feel like everyone else has it figured out and you're behind.

Almost everyone has an inner critic. For some people it's quiet. For others, it's loud and mean. Either way, it's not the truth about you.

What the inner critic sounds like

Tap each one.

📢The name-caller

"You're such an idiot." "You're so annoying." Harsh labels that stick.

🔮The fortune-teller

"You'll embarrass yourself." "Nobody will like you." Predicting the worst before anything happens.

📏The comparer

"Everyone's better than you." "You'll never be as good as them."

Why it's so loudIt thinks it's protecting you. It's not.
It thinks it's protecting you.
The inner critic often tries to keep you from getting hurt or embarrassed by getting to you first. Its methods are just terrible.
Teen brains are tuned to how others see you.
During the teen years, your brain pays extra attention to social judgment. That can make self-criticism louder.
Negative thoughts feel more true.
Brains have a negativity bias: bad stuff sticks more than good. One criticism can outweigh ten compliments.
It repeats what it's heard.
Sometimes the inner critic echoes things other people have said to you, including put-downs about your body, your skin, your accent, how you learn, or who you like. That doesn't make them true.
📱 The critic onlineTheir highlight reel isn't evidence against you.

Social media gives the inner critic a lot of material: likes, follower counts, filtered faces, highlight reels. Every scroll can become a comparison.

The worst part: the critic uses other people's best moments as evidence against you, and it never mentions that those moments are edited.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

You can talk back to it.

Tap one to see how.

🏷 Notice and name it.

"There's my inner critic again." Just noticing creates a little distance between you and the thought.

❓ Check the evidence.

Is this thought a fact or an opinion? What would I say if a friend thought this about themselves?

🫶 Talk to yourself like a friend.

Research on self-compassion shows that being kind to yourself after mistakes helps people feel better and try again. If you grew up hearing that being hard on yourself is how you get better, you can still aim high. Kindness gets you there too.

🔄 Find a fairer thought.

Not fake positive, just more accurate. "I'm an idiot" becomes "I made a mistake, and I can fix it."

📝 Keep a proof list.

Write down compliments, wins, and kind things people say. Read it when the critic gets loud.

Try itAnswer it like your best friend would.

Write down something your inner critic says. Then answer it the way a good friend would.

Try itSame moment, two voices. Which one's talking?

Which voice is talking?

You stumble over your words during a class presentation.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make the critic louder.
🙉 Trying to shut it up completely.

Fighting thoughts often makes them louder. Noticing and answering works better.

😁 Forced positivity.

"I'm amazing at everything!" doesn't feel believable. Aim for fair and kind instead.

🔁 Replaying mistakes at night.

If you can't stop, write it down and tell yourself you'll think about it tomorrow.

📏 Comparing to others.

Comparison is the critic's favorite tool. Take breaks from feeds that feed it.

When to get more help

  • The critic is loud most of the day, most days.
  • You feel worthless or hopeless.
  • You're having thoughts of hurting yourself.

These are signs to talk to someone soon. A counselor can help. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Plenty of characters have a mean voice in their heads. Watch what they do about it. A few of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

The Dot · Peter H. Reynolds, 2003 Throwback

Vashti is sure she can't draw. Her teacher asks her to make one mark and sign it. Then Vashti does something kind for a kid who says the same thing.

Fish in a Tree · Lynda Mullaly Hunt, 2015

Ally has believed for years that she's dumb because she can't read. A new teacher sees what the critic missed: she has dyslexia, and she's smart.

Heads up: Some classmates bully Ally.

🎬 Watch

Luca · Pixar, 2021

Alberto names the doubting voice in Luca's head Bruno and tells him to yell "Silenzio, Bruno!" That's notice-and-name-it. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

It's You I Like · Fred Rogers, 1971 Throwback

Mister Rogers liked you without the good grades and the right clothes. Try talking to yourself the way he talked to you.

Firework · Katy Perry, 2010

It starts with the critic's lines, about feeling like you don't count. Then it answers them.

✏️ Make your own

Draw your inner critic as a character, like Bruno. Give it a name and a face. Then draw your best friend next to it, with a speech bubble saying what they'd say back.

What this might mean

When the critic is this loud, it can feel like the truth. It isn't.

What might help right now

  • 🏷Name it: "That's my critic talking."
  • ✍️Use the talk-back exercise above.
  • 🗣Tell someone you trust what the voice is saying. Saying it out loud often shrinks it.

What this might mean

Comparison gives the critic its ammunition. You're seeing other people's outsides, not their insides.

What might help right now

  • 📵Take a break from the feed for a few hours.
  • 🪞The Comparison Trap has more on this.
  • 📝Write down one thing you're good at that has nothing to do with anyone else.

What this might mean

Mistakes feel huge in the moment. Most fade faster than you think.

What might help right now

  • 🫶Say: "I made a mistake. I'm still okay."
  • 🧰If someone was affected, The Repair Kit can help.
  • 🌙If it keeps you up, write it down and leave it for tomorrow.

Feeling low about yourself is heavy. You don't have to carry it alone.

What might help right now

  • 📝Read your proof list, or start one now with one thing.
  • 💬Reach out to someone who makes you feel good about yourself.
  • 🗣If this feeling hangs around, talk to a counselor.
