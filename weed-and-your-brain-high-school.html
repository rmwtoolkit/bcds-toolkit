Part 1 · Understand it

What THC does to a growing brain

Sound familiar?Forget both extremes. Here's the science.
  • People talk about weed like it's either totally harmless or totally ruins your life.
  • Someone said it's natural, so it must be safe.
  • You've seen pens, carts, dabs, and gummies in people's stories and at parties.
  • It's legal for adults where you live, and someone in your family uses it.
  • You've tried it, or you use it, and you're wondering what it's actually doing.
  • You're worried about a friend who seems high a lot.

You've probably heard both extremes. The real science is more interesting than either one, and you can handle it.

How many students actually use itMost of your grade isn't using.
This isn't the weed of the 1990sSame name. Way stronger.
Where THC lands in your brainTHC borrows your brain's own system.
The biggest U.S. study yetYour brain keeps leveling up. Weed can stall it.
What the research showsMemory, focus, mood, sleep.
🧠 Memory takes a hit.
While you're high, the hippocampus has trouble saving new memories. In regular users, memory and attention problems can hang around for days after the last use, and they usually get better after weeks without it.
🎯 Focus and follow-through.
THC makes it harder to plan, stay on task, and switch between things. Those skills live in the front of the brain, the last part to finish growing, around age 25.
🪝 Starting young makes it stickier.
Cannabis use disorder is real: wanting to cut back and not being able to. People who start before 18 are several times more likely to develop it than people who start as adults (CDC).
😰 It can make anxiety worse.
Lots of people use weed to chill. Strong weed often does the opposite: racing heart, paranoia, panic. Regular use is linked to more anxiety and depression over time.
😴 Sleep gets weird.
THC can help you fall asleep but cuts down REM sleep, the dreaming stage your brain uses to process and remember. Stop after regular use and sleep gets rough for a while, which is a big reason people find it hard to quit.
⚠️ Strong weed and psychosisStronger THC, bigger risk.

Psychosis means losing touch with what's real: hearing or seeing things that aren't there, or paranoid beliefs that won't let go. In a 2024 Ontario study, teens who used cannabis had about 11 times the odds of a psychotic disorder compared with teens who didn't. Risk climbs with higher potency, daily use, a younger start, and a family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder.

Most teens who use won't develop psychosis. But it's one of the clearest risks in the research, and it's tied to exactly what's changed: strength.

The honest part: nobody can tell in advance whose brain will react this way.

🍬 Edibles: the slow trapEdibles: slow to start, hard to stop.

The most common edible mistake: "It's not working," so they eat another one. Then both kick in.

The worst part: once it's eaten, there's no way to speed it up or stop it. You wait it out.

Two more things people don't knowThe stuff nobody mentions.
🤮 Throwing up for days (CHS).
Heavy, frequent use can cause cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome: waves of vomiting and stomach pain that keep coming back. The CDC reports ER visits for it rising fastest among people 15 to 24. The only real fix is stopping.
⛽ Delta-8 and "hemp" gummies are still THC.
Delta-8, THC-O, and "hemp-derived" gummies and vapes sold in gas stations and online still get you high. They're loosely regulated, and lab tests often find the label is wrong. About 9 in 100 seniors used them last year.
Part 2 · Practice the skills

Making your own call

What actually helps

Know your why. Have your line.

Tap one to see how.

🧭 Know your why.

If you're deciding about weed, notice what you're hoping it'll do: relax, sleep, fit in, try something new. Then ask what else gets you that.

💬 Have your line ready.

"I'm good." "It makes me paranoid." "I've got a game." "Not tonight." Nobody needs a speech.

🚗 Never drive or ride high.

THC slows reaction time and messes with judging distance. Call a parent, a friend who's sober, or a ride. Lots of families have a "pick me up, no questions tonight" deal. Ask for one.

🫂 Look after a friend who's too high.

Find a calm, quiet spot. Water, a snack, slow breathing. Remind them it will pass. Stay with them. If they have chest pain, can't stay awake, or something feels like an emergency, call 911.

📉 Cutting back? Expect a rough week.

Irritability, bad sleep, vivid dreams, and low appetite usually peak in the first week and fade over a couple of weeks. A counselor can help you plan for it.

🧑‍🏫 Talk to someone.

Counselors and nurses help students with this all the time. If you're worried about what stays private, ask that first.

What the research says

What the research says

Vape carts and dabs can be 60 to 90% THC, several times stronger than flower.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Which answer surprised you most? Why do you think so many people believe the opposite?
  • Where do most people our age get their information about weed?
  • If a younger sibling asked you about weed, what would you want them to know?
  • What makes it hard to say something to a friend who's using a lot?
Tip for the person leading

Keep questions about "people our age," never about "you." Nobody should have to share whether they use. If someone does share, thank them and move on without follow-up questions in the group.

Try itReal moments. What's your move?

Pick a move. There may be more than one good one.

Saturday night. Your friend ate an edible an hour ago, says nothing's happening, and reaches for a second one.

Your friend is high, panicking, and convinced their heart is about to explode.

Your ride home has been smoking all night and says they're fine to drive.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually backfire.
😱 Scare stories.

"One hit and your life is over" isn't true, and when people find that out, they stop trusting the real risks.

⚖️ "It's legal, so it's fine."

Alcohol is legal for adults too. The age limits exist because developing brains react differently.

🛌 Using it every night to sleep or calm down.

It works less over time, and stopping makes sleep and anxiety worse for a while.

👀 Comparing to friends who seem fine.

Changes in memory, motivation, and mood are hard to spot from the outside, or in yourself.

Get help right away if

  • Someone has chest pain, or their heart won't slow down.
  • Someone can't stay awake or can't be woken up.
  • Someone keeps vomiting and can't stop.
  • Someone is seeing or hearing things, or is so paranoid they could hurt themselves or someone else.
  • A little kid ate an edible.

Call 911 for an emergency. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. See Get help below.
