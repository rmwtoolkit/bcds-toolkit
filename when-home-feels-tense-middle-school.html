When Home Feels Tense

You can care without carrying it all.

What's going on

When home feels tense, it follows you to school: sleep, focus, friendships, and mood. Kids often wonder if they caused it or could fix it. Adult problems are adult responsibilities. It's not your fault.

Adults argue, or life changes→Nowhere to fully relax→It follows you to school→Acting fine at school→"Is it my fault?"

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "I have to pick a side."

🫀 Body

  • can't fall asleep
  • headaches
  • irritable, hard to focus

🏠 Situations

  • arguing you hear from your room
  • forgetting things at the other house
  • being asked to carry messages

Where to step in

Find steady ground.

  1. Find your calm spot: a corner, headphones, a walk, a friend's house.

Part 1 · Understand it

When home feels unsettled

Sound familiar?Hard stretches at home affect everything.
  • The adults at home argue a lot, and you can hear it from your room.
  • Your parents are separating or divorced, and everything is changing.
  • You split time between two homes and keep forgetting things at the other house.
  • Someone at home is sick, stressed about money, or going through something hard.
  • There's a new stepparent, partner, or sibling, and it's an adjustment.
  • You feel like you have to pick a side, or be the one who keeps the peace.

Every family goes through hard stretches, whether you're raised by two parents, one, grandparents, foster parents, two moms or two dads, or a big extended family. When home feels tense, it affects everything: sleep, focus, friendships, and mood.

What tense can look like

Tap each one.

⛈Conflict

Fighting, yelling, or the silent treatment between adults, or between you and someone at home.

🏠🏠Change

Divorce, two homes, moving, new family members. Even good changes take getting used to.

🎒Stress

Money worries, illness, a family member struggling. Everyone is on edge, even if no one talks about it.

Why it hits so hardIt's not your fault. Ever.
Home is supposed to be the safe base.
When home feels unsettled, it can feel like there's nowhere to fully relax.
It follows you to school.
Stress at home can make it hard to focus, sleep, or enjoy things. That's a normal reaction, not a weakness.
Kids often blame themselves.
It's common to wonder if you caused it or could fix it. Adult problems are adult responsibilities. It's not your fault.
It can be hard to talk about.
You might feel embarrassed, or worry about being disloyal to your family. In some families, keeping family business private matters a lot. You can respect that and still talk to one safe person. That isn't betrayal.
💛 You are not responsible for fixing itYou can care without carrying it all.

You can care about the people in your family without carrying their problems. Helping out, like watching a sibling or translating for a parent, can be part of loving your family. Fixing adult problems isn't your job. Your job right now is to be a kid, go to school, and take care of yourself.

The worst part: sometimes you have to act fine at school when things at home are anything but fine.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

You can't fix home. You can find steady ground.

Tap one to see how.

🛋 Find your calm spot.

A corner, headphones, a walk, the library, a friend's house. Somewhere you can take a breather when things get loud.

📓 Get it out.

Write, draw, play music. Feelings that stay bottled up tend to get bigger.

🧑‍🏫 Tell one adult.

A counselor, teacher, coach, relative, faith leader, or friend's parent. You don't have to share everything to get support.

🚫 Stay out of the middle.

If adults ask you to carry messages or pick sides, it's okay to say, "I don't want to be in the middle of this."

🗂 Make two homes work.

Keep a checklist for packing, a shared calendar, and duplicates of the basics if you can. Less to forget, less stress.

🌱 Keep your routines.

Sleep, school, activities, and friends give you something steady when home isn't.

Try itTwo tough nights. What would help you?

Pick the most helpful move.

The adults at home are arguing downstairs and you can't fall asleep.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make the stress heavier.
🦸 Trying to fix it all yourself.

It's too much for one person, especially a kid.

🙊 Pretending everything is fine.

Holding it in usually makes stress come out sideways: irritability, trouble sleeping, or headaches.

⚖️ Picking sides.

You get to love the people in your family even when they disagree.

📱 Venting about it publicly online.

Posting about family stuff can make things worse at home and stick around longer than the feeling.

Tell an adult right away if

  • Someone at home is hurting you, threatening you, or making you feel unsafe.
  • You see violence between adults at home.
  • You don't have enough food, or no one is taking care of you.
  • You're thinking about hurting yourself.
  • You're being put down or threatened at home for who you are, like your gender or who you like.

School adults are there to help keep you safe, and this is not your fault. The Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline is free and open 24/7: call or text 1-800-422-4453.

LGBTQ+ and home doesn't feel safe? Tell a counselor, or reach The Trevor Project: call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678. Trans Lifeline: 877-565-8860.

If you're in danger right now, call 911, or 988 can send a crisis team. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Every kind of family hits hard stretches. These stories get what that feels like. A couple might take you back.

Merci Suárez Changes Gears · Meg Medina, 2018

Merci's big family lives in three houses side by side. Then her grandfather Lolo starts forgetting things, and everyone is on edge. It's the stress kind of tense from the lesson. Winner of the Newbery Medal.

🎬 Watch

Inside Out · Pixar, 2015

Riley's family moves, her parents are stressed, and they bicker. Riley tries to act fine for them. That's the worst part the lesson talks about. Rated PG.

The Parent Trap · Nancy Meyers, 1998 Throwback

Twins split between two homes, one with each parent, find each other at summer camp. A comedy about two homes. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Three Little Birds · Bob Marley and the Wailers, 1977

"Every little thing gonna be all right." A good first song for a calm-spot playlist when it gets loud downstairs.

🖼️ Look

Sisters · Raina Telgemeier, 2014

Raina, her little sister, and their mom drive from California to Colorado while Dad flies in later. Something is off between the parents, and the girls feel it in the back seat. A graphic memoir.
