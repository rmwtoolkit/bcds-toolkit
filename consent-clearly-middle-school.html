Consent, Clearly

Asking first is how respect sounds.

What's going on

Consent means asking and respecting the answer. A real yes is given freely, can change anytime, and is clear. It applies to hugs, photos, phones, and more, with anyone of any gender.

Someone doesn't ask→Saying no feels rude→You go along→Going along looks like yes→Silence gets treated as yes

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "Saying no would be awkward."
  • "Sure, I guess."
  • "Come on, don't be boring."

👀 Signals to stop

  • pulling away
  • going quiet or freezing
  • looking uncomfortable

📱 Online

  • posting a photo without asking
  • screenshotting private messages
  • adding someone to a chat

Where to step in

Just ask. Then respect the answer.

  1. Just ask: "Can I post this?" "Want a hug?" It's awkward for two seconds.
  2. If someone hesitates, pulls back, or goes quiet, stop and check in.
  3. Take a no with "Okay, no problem." Practice yours: "No thanks." "Stop."

If your no wasn't respected, it's not your fault. RAINN, free and confidential, 24/7: 1-800-656-4673. In danger, call 911.

Part 1 · Understand it

What consent actually means

Sound familiar?Ever felt like you had to say yes?
  • Someone hugged you, tickled you, or grabbed you, and you didn't like it but didn't say anything.
  • A friend posted a photo of you that you hated, without asking.
  • Someone grabbed your phone and started reading your messages.
  • You weren't sure if a friend was okay with a joke or a game, or just going along.
  • You felt like you had to say yes because saying no would be awkward.
  • You've wondered what consent actually looks like in real life, not just in a health class definition.

Consent means asking and respecting the answer. It applies to everything from hugs to photos to phones to anything physical or romantic, with anyone of any gender.

What consent is

Tap each one.

🗣Asked and given freely

A real yes happens without pressure, guilt, or threats.

🔄Can change anytime

Saying yes once doesn't mean yes forever, or yes to everything. Anyone can change their mind, even partway through.

✨Clear and enthusiastic

Silence isn't a yes. "I guess" isn't really a yes. If you're not sure, ask, and look for a clear, happy yes.

Why it can feel complicatedGoing along isn't the same as yes.
Saying no can feel rude.
Lots of people are taught to be polite and go along, and some families expect kids to hug every relative. A wave or a high five is a kind way to say hi too. Your body, your stuff, and your image belong to you. Saying no is allowed.
Body language is real communication.
Pulling away, going quiet, looking uncomfortable, or freezing all mean stop, even without words. Not everyone shows it the same way, so when in doubt, ask.
Pressure isn't always loud.
"Come on, don't be boring" or asking over and over until someone gives in isn't consent. It's pressure.
It applies online too.
Posting photos, sharing screenshots, or adding people to group chats can all need consent.
📱 Consent onlineAsk before you post, share, or send.

Asking before posting a photo of someone. Not screenshotting private messages to share. Not adding someone to a group chat they'd hate. Not sending photos nobody asked for. Consent applies to screens too.

The worst part: once something is posted or sent without someone's okay, it can't really be taken back.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Asking is awkward for two seconds. Do it anyway.

Tap one to see how.

❓ Just ask.

"Can I post this?" "Want a hug?" "Is this okay?" Asking feels awkward for about two seconds, and it shows respect.

👀 Watch for signals.

If someone hesitates, pulls back, goes quiet, or looks uncomfortable, stop and check in.

🛑 Accept no gracefully.

"Okay, no problem" is the best answer to a no. No sulking, no pushing, no guilt trips.

🗣 Practice saying no.

"No thanks." "I'm not into that." "Stop." You don't owe anyone a reason or an apology.

🔄 Check in again.

Things change. Asking "Still good?" is part of respecting someone.

🧑‍🏫 Tell someone if your no wasn't respected.

If someone ignored your no, it's not your fault, and a trusted adult can help.

Try itThree moments. Is that a real yes?

Tap an answer for each one.

Your friend says "sure, I guess" when you ask to post a photo, but looks unhappy.

Someone says yes to a hug, and then a minute later says they want to stop.

Someone keeps asking after being told no, until the other person finally says "fine."

Try itSomeone said stop. What's your move?

Pick the best move.

A group of friends keeps tickling a kid who's laughing but also saying "stop!"

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpGuessing isn't asking. These don't work.
🤷 Assuming silence means yes.

No answer is not a yes. Ask.

😒 Guilt-tripping after a no.

"Wow, okay, whatever" turns a no into pressure.

🙈 Ignoring body language.

Freezing or pulling back says a lot, even without words.

🍺 Thinking alcohol changes the rules.

Being drunk or high means someone can't give consent. It's not an excuse for anyone.

Get help if

  • Someone touched you or did something sexual without your consent.
  • Someone is pressuring or threatening you to do something physical.
  • You're worried about something that happened to a friend.

It's not your fault, no matter what, even if it was someone you know or someone in your family. RAINN is free and confidential, 24/7: call 1-800-656-4673. A school counselor can help too.

If you're in danger, call 911. You can also call or text 988 any time. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Asking first shows up in more stories than you'd think. One of these you probably watched a hundred times.

📖 Read

Don't Hug Doug (He Doesn't Like It) · Carrie Finison, art by Daniel Wiseman, 2021

Doug likes high fives, not hugs. The book's rule is simple: ask first, because everybody gets to decide. It's a picture book, so it's a good one to read to a younger cousin.

🎬 Watch

Frozen · Disney, 2013 Throwback

At the end, Kristoff stumbles over his words trying to ask Anna if he can kiss her. Awkward for two seconds, and she says yes. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Brave · Sara Bareilles, 2013

A song about saying what you mean out loud. Play it before you practice saying no.

Respect · Aretha Franklin, 1967

Your grandparents know this one. Aretha spells it out, letter by letter.

🖼️ Look

Consent (for Kids!) · Rachel Brian, 2020

A short graphic book on boundaries from the artist behind the famous consent animations. It's written for younger kids, and the comics make the rules easy to remember.
