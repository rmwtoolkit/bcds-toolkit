Study Smarter

Familiar isn't the same as remembered.

What's going on

Most students were never taught how to study. Rereading feels like learning because things look familiar. Testing yourself feels harder, and that struggle is your brain building stronger memories.

You reread your notes→It all looks familiar→You feel ready→You blank on the test→You cram all night→It's gone by next week

Part 1 · Understand it

How learning really works

Sound familiar?Studied for hours. Still blanked.
  • You reread your notes three times, felt ready, and then blanked on the test.
  • You highlighted almost the entire page.
  • You studied for hours the night before and forgot most of it by the next week.
  • Studying with friends turned into talking with friends.
  • You feel like you study more than other people and get less out of it.

Most students were never taught how to study. The good news: the methods that work best are known, and they often take less time.

What the research says works

Tap each one.

🧠Pulling it out

Testing yourself, without looking, is one of the strongest ways to remember something. It's called retrieval practice.

📆Spreading it out

Short sessions over several days beat one long session. It's called spaced practice.

🔀Mixing it up

Practicing different types of problems together, instead of all of one kind, helps you learn when to use what.

Why the usual way doesn't workFamiliar isn't the same as remembered.
Rereading feels like learning.
When you reread, things look familiar, so your brain says "I know this." But recognizing something isn't the same as being able to recall it on a test.
Highlighting is mostly coloring.
Research on highlighting finds it does little on its own. Deciding what's important helps a bit. Testing yourself on it helps a lot more.
Cramming fades fast.
Cramming can get you through tomorrow's quiz, but most of it is gone in a few days. That's why finals feel like learning it all over again.
Hard is good.
Testing yourself feels harder than rereading. That struggle is the feeling of your brain building stronger memories.
📱 Studying with your phone nearbyForty focused minutes beat two distracted hours.

Texts and notifications break up study time into tiny pieces. Even quick checks pull your attention away, and it takes a while to get it back.

The worst part: two distracted hours can teach you less than 40 focused minutes, and they feel twice as exhausting.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Quiz yourself. Spread it out. Sleep.

Tap one to see how.

🙈 Close the book and recall.

Read a section, then close it and write or say everything you remember. Then check what you missed.

🃏 Make practice questions.

Turn notes into questions. Flashcards, practice tests, or just covering the answer and quizzing yourself.

📆 Start a few days early.

Three 20-minute sessions on different days beat one hour the night before.

👩‍🏫 Teach it.

Explain it out loud to a friend, a grandparent, a little sibling, or your dog, in any language you speak. If you can teach it, you know it.

🔀 Mix your practice.

Do a few of each kind of math problem instead of 20 of the same.

😴 Sleep on it.

Sleep is when your brain locks in what you learned. A night of sleep is part of studying.

Try itClose the book. What do you remember?

Pick something you're studying. Read it once, then hide it and write down everything you remember here. Then look back and see what you missed. That gap is what to study next.

Build a study planFour short sessions beat one long night.

Test coming up? Spread it out:

  1. 4 days before: Make practice questions from your notes. Try them.
  2. 3 days before: Recall check on the hardest topics.
  3. 2 days before: Practice test, mixed topics, no notes. Check answers.
  4. Night before: Short review of what you missed. Then sleep.
Try itTwo tests coming. Pick the method.

Which one would help most?

You have a vocab quiz Friday. It's Tuesday.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese feel like studying but don't stick.
📖 Rereading as your main method.

Fine as a first step. Not enough on its own.

🖍 Highlighting everything.

If everything is highlighted, nothing is.

🌙 Cramming all night.

Trading sleep for study time usually backfires. Sleep helps memory.

📱 Studying with your phone face up.

Every buzz costs you focus. Out of sight works better.

When to ask for more help

  • You study hard and still struggle in a class.
  • Reading or focusing feels much harder for you than for others. Dyslexia, ADHD, and other learning differences are common, and support helps.
  • You're still learning English and the reading takes extra time.
  • School stress is taking over your evenings or your sleep.

Teachers, learning specialists, and counselors can help. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. A couple of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

Your Fantastic Elastic Brain · JoAnn Deak, illustrated by Sarah Ackerley, 2010 Throwback

A picture book about how your brain grows when you stretch it, and why mistakes are part of how it learns. That's the lesson's "hard is good" idea, drawn for little kids.

Fish in a Tree · Lynda Mullaly Hunt, 2015

Ally hides that reading is hard for her until a new teacher figures out she has dyslexia and finds a way of learning that works for her brain. It's the lesson's "when to ask for more help" part, as a story.

Heads up: A mean classmate picks on Ally, and some boys push around her friend Albert.

🎬 Watch

Akeelah and the Bee · Doug Atchison, 2006

Akeelah gets thousands of flashcards to learn. She skips rope while she spells, and half her neighborhood ends up quizzing her. That's retrieval practice with a whole team behind it. Rated PG.

Inside Out · Pixar, 2015 Throwback

Every night while Riley sleeps, the day's memories get sent off to long-term memory. The lesson says the same thing: a night of sleep is part of studying. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Try Everything · Shakira, 2016 Throwback

The Zootopia song. Keep making mistakes, keep trying. Testing yourself feels worse than rereading, and that's the point.

✏️ Make your own

Make five flashcards for something you're studying right now. Question on the front, answer on the back. Quiz yourself today, then again in two days, and put a check on each card you get without peeking.

Ratings checked October 2026. Not sure a pick is right for you? Ask a parent, a teacher, or your counselor.
