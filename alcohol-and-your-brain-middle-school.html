Alcohol and Your Brain

Judgment leaves first. Breathing goes last.

What's going on

Alcohol shuts your brain down, front to back. A drink or two quiets the part that says "maybe not." Keep drinking, and it reaches memory, then balance, then breathing. Only time sobers you up.

A drink or two→"Maybe not" goes quiet→Memory stops saving→Balance and speech slip→Breathing slows→"Let them sleep it off"

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "I feel totally fine."
  • "It's basically fizzy water."
  • "Don't wake up your mom."

🆘 Signs to call 911

  • can't be woken
  • puking while passed out
  • slow or irregular breathing

🔁 Situations

  • a sip at a family party
  • a bottle from a parent's cabinet
  • drinking posted like a flex

Where to step in

Know CUPS. Call every time.

  1. CUPS: cold skin, unresponsive, puking while passed out, slow breathing. Any one means call 911.
  2. Roll them on their side, top knee bent, and stay with them until help comes.
  3. Agree on a code word with an adult you trust, so you can leave.

Call 911 right away if someone can't be woken up, or keeps passing out.

Part 1 · Understand it

What alcohol does to a growing brain

Sound familiar?The party drug nobody explains.
  • Adults around you drink, and it seems totally normal.
  • Someone offered you a sip at a family party.
  • You've seen older kids post about drinking like it's a flex.
  • You've wondered how much is "too much."
  • You've seen someone act really different after drinking.
  • You're curious what being drunk actually feels like.

Alcohol is the most common substance at parties, and the one people know the least about. Families have different rules about it too. Some drink at holidays or religious ceremonies, and some never drink, for faith, health, or family history. Whatever your family's way, here's what alcohol actually does.

How many students actually drinkMost students aren't drinking.
Alcohol shuts your brain down, front to backJudgment leaves first. Breathing goes last.
☕ The sobering-up mythsOnly time sobers you up.

Coffee, cold showers, eating, throwing up, walking it off, sleeping it off. None of them lower the alcohol in someone's blood. Coffee makes a drunk person a wide-awake drunk person. Only time works.

The scary one: "just let them sleep it off." Alcohol can keep rising while someone sleeps, and a person who vomits while passed out can choke.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Looking out for yourself and your friends

What actually helps

Know CUPS. Call every time.

Tap one to see how.

🆘 Know the signs: CUPS.

Cold, clammy, pale, or bluish skin. Unresponsive: can't be woken. Puking while passed out. Slow or irregular breathing, fewer than 8 breaths a minute or long gaps. Any one of these means call 911.

📞 Call. Every time.

If someone might have alcohol poisoning, call 911 or get an adult right away. Being in trouble is temporary. This might not be.

🛟 Recovery position.

Roll them on their side, top knee bent, head tilted so anything comes out instead of in. Stay with them until help comes. Don't leave them alone to sleep.

🚪 Have an exit plan.

Agree on a code word with a parent, caregiver, or another adult you trust: text it, and they call with an "emergency" so you can leave.

🥤 Watch your cup.

Hold onto your own drink, and don't take one you didn't see poured. That goes for soda too.

🧑‍🏫 Talk to someone.

Worried about your drinking, a friend's, or someone's at home? Counselors deal with this all the time.

Try itCoffee? Cold shower? Pick one.

Tap the one that actually works.

👥 In a groupOne sleepover. Three choices. You vote.

👥 For advisory or class · about 10 minutes · no materials needed

  1. Read the story out loud. Sam is sleeping over at Riley's house with two other friends.
  2. At each stop, everyone votes with fingers: 1, 2, or 3. No talking until the vote.
  3. Ask a couple of people why they picked what they picked. Then tap to see what usually happens.
👥 The sleepover · 1 of 3Stop 1 · 10:00pm

Riley pulls a bottle out of a backpack. "I took it from my parents' cabinet. They'll never notice."

  1. Try some so you're not the only one
  2. Say "I'm good" and put a movie on
  3. Text a parent your code word to get picked up
What usually happens

2 and 3 both work. Saying it like it's no big deal usually makes it no big deal. A movie or a snack run changes the plan for everyone.

👥 The sleepover · 2 of 3Stop 2 · 10:45pm

Riley has had a lot of it, fast. "See? I feel totally fine."

  1. Believe Riley
  2. Get Riley some water and stay close
  3. Ignore it and go to sleep
What usually happens

2. Alcohol keeps rising for a while after the last sip. Fine right now can turn into very not fine an hour later, and a smaller, younger body gets there faster.

👥 The sleepover · 3 of 3Stop 3 · 11:30pm

Riley threw up and now is really hard to wake up. Someone says, "Don't wake up your mom. We'll all get in so much trouble."

  1. Let Riley sleep it off
  2. Wake up Riley's parent right now
  3. Look it up online first
What usually happens

2, right now. Roll Riley on their side and get an adult. If Riley won't wake up or is breathing slowly, call 911. Being in trouble is temporary. This might not be.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Which stop was the hardest? What made it hard?
  • Why is it so hard to wake up a parent, even when you're worried?
  • What could Sam say at Stop 1 that wouldn't feel awkward?
Tip for the person leading

Make the answer at Stop 3 completely clear: wake up an adult, and call 911 if someone can't be woken. Keep it about Sam and Riley, not anyone in the room.

Try itReal moments. What's your move?

Pick a move. There may be more than one good one.

At a family party, a cousin offers you a sip of their drink when no adult is looking.

A friend's older brother offers you a hard seltzer. "It's basically fizzy water."

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

Call 911 right away if someone

  • Can't be woken up, or keeps passing out.
  • Has slow, irregular, or gasping breathing.
  • Has cold, clammy, pale, or bluish skin.
  • Vomits while passed out, or has a seizure.
  • Mixed alcohol with pills or other drugs.

Roll them on their side and stay with them. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. A few of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

Because of Winn-Dixie · Kate DiCamillo, 2000 Throwback

Gloria Dump hangs her old bottles on a tree so she won't forget the years she lost to drinking. Opal's mom drank too. Nobody in this book pretends it was nothing, and nobody says it was Opal's fault.

Heads up: A parent who left because of drinking. Told gently.

🎬 Watch

Inside Out · Pixar, 2015 Throwback

When Joy and Sadness get pulled out of Headquarters, the ones left at the console come up with a bad plan: run away. Alcohol does something like that. It shuts off the part that says "maybe not" first. Rated PG.

Pinocchio · Disney, 1940 Throwback

On Pleasure Island the boys are handed cigars and beer for free, and Jiminy Cricket, the voice that says "maybe not," gets left behind. Then come the donkey ears. Rated G.

🎧 Listen

Lean on Me · Bill Withers, 1972

"Just call on me." If a friend shows any of the CUPS signs, that's the rule: you call, every time.

🖼️ Look

Sunny Side Up · Jennifer L. Holm and Matthew Holm, 2015

Sunny is sent to her grandpa's in Florida for the summer, and slowly we find out why: her brother's drinking and drug use. The authors are siblings, and they've said it comes from their own family. A graphic novel.

Heads up: Sunny's older brother's drinking and drug use get worse, and the family fights about it. The ending is hopeful.

✏️ Make your own

Make your exit plan card. Pick your code word, write the name of the adult you'd text, and draw the phone. Keep it somewhere you'll see it before a sleepover.
