Perfect Is a Trap

Good enough is usually great.

What's going on

Wanting to do well is great. Needing everything to be perfect is exhausting, and it usually gets in the way. Under perfectionism there's often a fear of disappointing people or not being good enough.

You want to do well→It has to be perfect→Mistakes feel like proof→Good results feel like relief→Redo it or avoid it

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "I should have gotten 100."
  • "It's still not good enough."
  • "Everyone's doing better."

🫀 Body

  • stressed and tired
  • staying up late perfecting
  • relief instead of joy

🔁 Behaviors

  • redoing it three times
  • not raising your hand
  • replaying every mistake

Part 1 · Understand it

What perfectionism really is

Sound familiar?A 94 shouldn't feel like failing.
  • You got a 94 and all you could think about was the 6 points you missed.
  • You redid an assignment three times and it still didn't feel good enough.
  • You didn't try out, raise your hand, or turn something in because it might not be perfect.
  • You feel like you have to be the best at everything: school, sports, music, everything.
  • Mistakes feel like proof that something is wrong with you.
  • People call you a high achiever, but inside it feels more like fear.

Wanting to do well is great. Needing everything to be perfect is exhausting, and it usually gets in the way of doing well.

How perfectionism shows up

Tap each one.

🔁Overdoing it

Spending hours on things that are already good. Rewriting, re-checking, never feeling finished.

🙈Avoiding it

Not starting, not trying, not turning it in, because if you don't try, you can't fail.

🗡Beating yourself up

Every mistake replays in your head. Good results don't feel good, they just feel like relief.

Why it's so hard to let goIt feels like it's working. Until it isn't.
It feels like it's working.
Perfectionism can get results, so it feels necessary. But the cost (stress, sleep, joy) keeps climbing.
It's often about fear.
Under perfectionism is usually a fear: of disappointing people, of not being good enough, of being found out. For some kids it's also about making a family's hard work count, or feeling like you have to be twice as good because you're one of the only kids like you in the room.
Everyone's highlight reel is visible.
Seeing other people's achievements, online and at school, can make it feel like everyone is doing more and doing it better.
Research shows it's increasing.
Studies have found perfectionism has risen among young people over recent decades. If you feel this pressure, you're far from alone.
📱 Perfect onlineYour everyday vs. their highlight reel.

Feeds are full of perfect photos, perfect grades, perfect college acceptances. Filters and editing make it worse. It can feel like everyone else has it together and you're the only one struggling.

The worst part: you're comparing your messy, everyday life to everyone else's best moments, carefully chosen and edited.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Good enough is usually great.

Tap one to see how.

✅ Aim for good enough.

Ask: what would an A-minus version look like? Or a B version? Often it's the same quality in half the time.

⏲ Set a time limit.

Decide how long something deserves before you start. When time's up, you're done.

🧪 Try a small imperfection on purpose.

Send the text with a typo. Turn in the assignment without the extra round of edits. Notice that nothing terrible happens.

🗣 Talk back to the all-or-nothing voice.

"If it's not perfect, it's a failure" isn't true. Most things land somewhere in between, and that's fine.

🎉 Notice what went right.

After a test or game, name two things that went well before you look at what didn't.

🫶 Treat yourself like a friend.

Research on self-compassion shows that being kind to yourself after mistakes helps you bounce back and try again.

Try itWhat would good enough look like?

Think of something you're working on and can't seem to finish.

Try itTwo thoughts. Which one is the trap?

Which thought is the perfectionism trap talking?

You missed one question on a quiz.

Healthy striving says "I want to do well." Perfectionism says "I have to, or I'm not okay."

What doesn't actually helpMore pressure won't make it better.
🔁 Endless redoing.

There's a point where more effort stops making things better and only makes you more tired.

🙈 Not trying.

Avoiding things so you can't fail also means you can't succeed, learn, or have fun.

📏 Comparing yourself to everyone.

There will always be someone who seems to be doing more. Comparison keeps the bar moving.

🗡 Harsh self-talk.

Being mean to yourself doesn't make you better. It makes you more afraid to try.

When to get more help

  • Fear of mistakes is keeping you from sleeping, eating, or enjoying anything.
  • You feel like a failure most of the time, even when things go well.
  • You're having thoughts about hurting yourself when things aren't perfect.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Good enough is usually great. These stories prove it. A few of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

Ish · Peter H. Reynolds, 2004 Throwback

Ramon's brother laughs at his drawing, so he crumples every picture until his little sister shows him she's been saving them. A vase-ish vase is still a vase.

Mistakes That Worked · Charlotte Foltz Jones, 1991

The stories behind forty things invented or named by accident, like Post-it Notes, potato chips and Velcro. Turns out a lot of the world is a happy imperfection.

🎬 Watch

Encanto · Disney, 2021

Isabela makes perfect flowers every day. When she finally makes a prickly, lopsided cactus, she loves it. Rated PG.

Kiki's Delivery Service · Studio Ghibli, 1989

Kiki works so hard at her new job that she loses her magic. Her artist friend Ursula tells her to rest, and the magic comes back. Rated G.

🎧 Listen

Try Everything · Shakira, 2016

The Zootopia song. Judy's plan is to keep making new mistakes and keep trying anyway.

✏️ Make your own

Make something fast and imperfect on purpose. Set a timer for ten minutes and draw, build, or write it ish. When time's up, you're done. Sign it.
