Part 1 · Understand it

What's really going on

Sound familiar?Your feed found your insecurities fast. That's its job.
  • Your feed went from funny videos to guys explaining your "jawline score" in about a week.
  • You've checked your side profile in a mirror or a photo more times than you'd admit.
  • Someone in your grade got way taller over the summer and you started worrying about your height.
  • You started working out and now you feel guilty on days you don't.
  • A friend keeps talking about protein powder or pre-workout like it's magic.
  • You've asked yourself, "Am I ugly?" and an app or a comment section was happy to answer.

Lots of students feel this, whatever their gender or body type, and it's mostly by design. A lot of this content is built to make you feel behind, so you keep watching and buying.

The words you keep hearingNew words, same old trick: find a flaw.
Looksmaxing
Trying to max out how good you look. It can mean a haircut and sleep. Online, it often means ranking every part of your face.
Mogging
Looking so much better than someone that they "lose." It turns every photo into a contest.
Mewing
Pressing your tongue to the roof of your mouth to reshape your jaw. There's no good evidence it changes your bones.
Bone smashing
Tapping or hitting your face with hard objects to "build" bone. It can break bones and damage nerves. Doctors say don't.
Rating and "PSL"
Scoring faces with numbers. A number can't tell you if someone is funny, kind, or a good friend.
Bigorexia
Feeling like you're never muscular enough, even when you're strong. The clinical name is muscle dysmorphia, and it's real.
💸 Follow the moneyFirst they find a flaw. Then they sell the fix.

A lot of looksmax and gym creators sell something: a course, a jaw trainer, a supplement, a "glow up" plan. The easiest way to sell a fix is to convince you that you're broken first.

Rating videos also get huge numbers of comments, because people argue about the scores. The app shows you more of whatever keeps you arguing.

The worst part: the more you watch, the more "flaws" you learn to see in yourself. Most of them you never noticed before.

Your body is on its own schedule.

Tap each one.

📏Growth

Some kids hit puberty at 10, some at 15. Both are normal. Where you are in 7th grade says almost nothing about where you'll end up.

💪Muscle

Your body builds muscle much more easily after your growth spurt. If the guy next to you gains faster, it's mostly timing, not effort.

🧬Genes

Your bone structure, like your skin, hair, and eyes, comes from your family's genes. Rating videos treat one narrow look as the "ideal," and that's a trend, not a truth. No video, tongue trick, or app is going to change it.

Gym culture: the good and the grindTrain for you, not for the camera.
👍 The good
Lifting, sports, and training with friends are great for your body, your mood, and your sleep. Pediatricians say strength training is safe for kids your age with good form and supervision.
😬 The grind
When missing a workout feels like failing, when you can't skip a session even when you're sick or hurt, or when your mood depends on the mirror, the gym is running you.
📸 The physique online
Many bodies you see online are lit, posed, pumped up right before the photo, or filtered. Some are on steroids and don't say so.
What's in the shaker?Supplements aren't checked before they're sold.
🥤 Protein powder
Most teens get enough protein from regular food. Powder is a convenience, not a requirement.
⚡ Pre-workout and energy drinks
Often packed with a lot of caffeine. That can mean a racing heart, shaky hands, and bad sleep, which hurts your training.
💊 "Natural" supplements
Supplements don't have to be approved by the FDA before they're sold. Some have been found to contain ingredients that aren't on the label.
💉 Steroids and SARMs
These are drugs, not supplements. They are illegal to use without a prescription and can stop you from growing taller.
🍽 When eating turns into a rule bookBoys get eating disorders too.

Bulking, cutting, skipping meals to "get shredded," or eating only "clean" foods can slide into an eating disorder. People often picture eating disorders as a girl thing. Boys and men get them too, and they often get missed because the behavior looks like dedication.

Signs to take seriously: panic about missing a meal plan, hiding how you eat, feeling dizzy or cold all the time, or exercising to "undo" food.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Taking your mirror back

What actually helps

Boring habits beat secret tricks.

Tap one to see how.

🎯 Train for something you can do.

Pick goals about what your body does: run a mile faster, do your first pull up, make the team, or whatever fits your body. These feel better than goals about how it looks.

🧹 Clean up your feed.

Tap "not interested" on rating and "fix your face" videos. Follow athletes for skills, not physiques. The algorithm learns fast.

🛑 Ask "what are they selling?"

Before you believe a creator, find the link in their bio. If they profit from you feeling ugly, take that into account.

😴 Do the boring stuff.

Sleep, real meals, water, and showing up consistently do more for how you look and feel than any trick online.

🪞 Limit mirror checks.

Checking a "flaw" over and over makes it look bigger. Try setting a rule: one look before you leave, then done.

🧑‍⚕️ Ask a real expert.

A doctor, athletic trainer, or coach who knows you beats a stranger with a shirtless profile picture.

Try itMewing, steroids, supplements. Myth or fact?

Tap your answer. No score, just checking.

Mewing can reshape your jaw if you do it long enough.

Strength training is safe for middle and high schoolers when it's supervised and done with good form.

If a supplement is sold at a big store, it's been tested and approved.

Steroids can stop you from growing taller.

Boys don't really get eating disorders.

  2. Everyone votes with a hand signal: thumbs up for "useful," thumbs down for "sales pitch," flat hand for "not sure."
  3. Then the leader taps the claim to show what's actually going on.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Why do you think rating videos get so many views?
  • What's one thing about growing up you wish videos were more honest about?
  • What would a healthier version of gym culture at our school look like?
Tip for the person leading

Keep the talk on the content, not anyone's body. Nobody should be asked to rate, describe, or compare themselves or anyone in the room.

Try itYour friend wants to try it. What do you say?

Pick the move you'd want a friend to make.

A friend sends you a video about bone smashing and says he's going to try it tonight.

You've been working out every day. You get sick, and the idea of skipping makes you anxious.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What to do about the feed right nowMute the raters. Your face isn't a test.
🔕 Mute the raters.
Mute or block accounts that score faces or bodies. You'll stop seeing your own face as a list of problems.
🔁 Reset your recommendations.
Most apps let you reset or refresh your feed in settings. It's a quick fresh start.
📵 No rating apps.
"Rate my face" apps and AI raters are built to keep you coming back. The score changes with lighting and angle.
🙅 Don't post for ratings.
Posting your face or body for strangers to score invites cruelty, and screenshots last.

If it gets heavy

  • You're skipping meals, making yourself throw up, or exercising to make up for eating.
  • You or a friend are using steroids, SARMs, or anything you'd hide from a doctor.
  • You hate how you look so much it's hard to go to school or see friends.
  • You have thoughts of hurting yourself or not wanting to be here.

Tell a counselor, the health office, or a trusted adult. You can also call or text 988, any time, day or night.

If some of how you feel about your body is about your gender, that deserves real support too. A counselor can help, and so can Trans Lifeline (877-565-8860) or The Trevor Project.
