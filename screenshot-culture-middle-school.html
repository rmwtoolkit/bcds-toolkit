Part 1 · Understand it

How things get shared

Sound familiar?Private chats don't always stay private.
  • Something you said in a private chat ended up in front of people it wasn't meant for.
  • You screenshotted something funny and sent it around, then realized it might hurt someone.
  • A video of you doing something silly got passed around the grade.
  • You posted something in the moment and regretted it later.
  • Your location was on, and people knew where you were.
  • You've wondered what a coach, teacher, or future school might find if they searched your name.

Almost everything you do online can be saved, copied, and shared. That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to be thoughtful.

How things travel

Tap each one.

📸Screenshots

Disappearing messages don't really disappear. Anyone can screenshot, screen record, or photograph another screen.

🔁Forwarding

Something sent to one person can reach hundreds in minutes. Once it's out, you can't pull it back.

🗄Your footprint

Posts, comments, tags, and likes add up to a picture of you that can last for years.

Why it mattersAnyone can hit screenshot.
Private isn't always private.
"Close friends," private accounts, and disappearing messages all depend on everyone else choosing not to share.
Context gets lost.
A joke between friends can look very different to people who weren't there.
Some sharing hurts people.
Passing around someone's private message, embarrassing photo, or secret can do real harm, even if it seemed funny. Sharing who someone likes, that they're LGBTQ+, a health condition, or their family's immigration status can put them at real risk.
Some sharing breaks the law.
Sharing private or undressed photos of anyone can get you in serious trouble, even if someone else took them.
🔒 Your footprint can work for you tooPost things you'll be proud of later.

Your online presence isn't only a risk. Sharing your art, your sports highlights, a cause you care about, or kindness toward others can build a footprint you're proud of.

The worst part: the post you make when you're angriest, saddest, or trying hardest to be funny is often the one you'll most want back.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Pause first. Post second.

Tap one to see how.

⏸ Pause before you post or send.

Take ten seconds. If you're upset, take ten minutes, or until tomorrow.

👵 Use the billboard test.

Would you be okay with this on a billboard outside school, with your name on it? If not, don't post it.

🙅 Don't share what isn't yours.

Someone else's private message, photo, or secret isn't yours to pass on. Ask first, or don't share.

⚙️ Check your settings.

Make accounts private, turn off location sharing (or use Ghost Mode), and review who can tag you.

🧹 Clean up old posts.

Look back through your accounts and archive or delete anything you wouldn't post today.

📸 If it's about you, document it.

If someone shares something harmful about you, screenshot it for evidence, then report it and tell an adult.

Try itWould you put it on a billboard?

Got something you're thinking about posting or sending? Run it through these questions. Nothing is saved.

Try itIt lands in your chat. What's your move?

Pick the best move.

A friend sends you a screenshot of a classmate's private message that says they have a crush on someone. "Send it to the group!"

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

If something of yours gets sharedIt's not your fault. You don't have to handle it alone.
  1. Breathe. It feels huge right now. Most things blow over faster than you think.
  2. Document it. Screenshot where it's posted, who shared it, and when.
  3. Report it. Every major app has a way to report content. Use it.
  4. Tell an adult. A parent or caregiver, counselor, teacher, or another adult you trust can help, especially if it's harassment or a private image. If it's about something you haven't told your family, a counselor can help you figure out who to tell.
  5. Get support. Lean on friends who have your back.

If a sexual image of you (under 18) was shared, NCMEC's free Take It Down service can help remove it from participating sites. A trusted adult can help you use it.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make it worse.
👻 Trusting disappearing messages.

Screenshots exist. Assume anything you send could be saved.

🔥 Posting while angry.

Angry posts often become the ones you regret most.

🤐 Hiding it when something goes wrong.

Asking for help early makes things easier to fix.

😱 Panicking about your whole footprint.

Everyone has posted something cringey. Clean up what you can and be thoughtful going forward.

Get help right away if

  • Someone shares, or threatens to share, private or sexual images of you.
  • Someone is using screenshots or information to blackmail or threaten you.
  • You're being harassed or targeted online.

This is not your fault, and you don't have to handle it alone. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Long before screenshots, people were finding out that private words don't always stay private. These stories get it.

📖 Read

Harriet the Spy · Louise Fitzhugh, 1964

Harriet writes brutally honest notes about her classmates in a private notebook. Then she loses it, and they read every word. A good one for the billboard test.

Heads up: Her classmates gang up on her for a while, and the family's nanny leaves.

Lilly's Purple Plastic Purse · Kevin Henkes, 1996 Throwback

Lilly gets mad at her teacher and slips a mean drawing of him into his bag. Then she wishes she could take it back. The post you make when you're angriest, picture-book edition.

🎬 Watch

Ralph Breaks the Internet · Disney, 2018

Ralph's videos go viral, and then he reads the comments. Watch what one mean line does to him, and how fast things spread online. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Stand by You · Rachel Platten, 2015

A promise to stick with someone when things get rough. Think of the friend whose private message got passed around, and what "I've got you" would mean to them.

🖼️ Look

Invisible Emmie · Terri Libenson, 2017

Shy Emmie writes a sappy love note as a joke, drops it, and the class clown shows it to everyone. Told half in comics, half in text.

✏️ Make your own

Draw the billboard outside your school with your name on it. Put something on it you'd be proud of: your art, a highlight, something kind you did. That's the footprint working for you.
