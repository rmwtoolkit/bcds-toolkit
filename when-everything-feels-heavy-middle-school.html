Part 1 · Understand it

What depression is, and how it works

Sound familiar?If this sounds familiar, you're not alone.
  • Stuff you used to love feels kind of pointless.
  • You're tired all the time, even after a full night's sleep.
  • Little things make you snap at people.
  • Homework that used to be easy feels impossible to start.
  • You want to be alone, but being alone feels bad too.
  • You feel more empty than sad.

Everyone has days like this. When they stack up for weeks, it might be depression. Depression is common, it's nobody's fault, and it gets better with help.

You're far from the only oneAbout 4 in 10 high schoolers have felt this.
A bad week, or depression?Sad comes and goes. Depression sticks around.
😢 Sadness
Happens because of something, like a loss or a fight. It comes and goes. You can still laugh and enjoy things.
🌧 Depression
Sticks around most of the day, almost every day, for two weeks or more. It changes your sleep, energy, appetite, and how you see yourself.
😠 It doesn't always look sad.
In kids and teens, depression often looks like being cranky, angry, or bored with everything.
🎭 It can hide.
Lots of people with depression still go to school, crack jokes at lunch, and post like everything's fine. Then they get home and crash.

What depression does

Tap each one.

🔋Drains your battery

Everything takes more energy, even showering or answering a text.

🎮Turns down the fun

Things that used to feel good feel flat.

🌫Fogs your thinking

Focusing, remembering, and making decisions get harder. Grades often slip.

😴Messes with sleep and appetite

Some people sleep way more, some can't sleep. Some eat more, some barely eat.

🗯Changes the story

It tells you things like "Nobody cares" or "It'll always be like this."

🧬 Why it happensNot weakness. Not laziness. Not your fault.

There's no single cause. It can come from genes, stress, loss, being bullied, feeling left out, not sleeping enough, or sometimes no reason you can point to. It isn't weakness, it isn't laziness, and you didn't choose it.

The good news: depression is one of the most treatable health conditions there is.

The downward spiralWaiting to feel like it keeps you stuck.
Part 2 · Practice the skills

Climbing out, one small step at a time

Action comes firstMotivation shows up after you start.
What actually helps

Small steps beat big plans.

Tap one to see how.

🪜 Do it before you feel like it.

Shrink the task until it's almost silly: put on your shoes, open the doc, reply to one text. Then let yourself stop or keep going.

🚶 Move your body.

Walking, shooting hoops, dancing in your room. Moving is one of the best things you can do for your mood.

🤝 Stay connected, even a little.

Depression wants you to hide. Send one text. Sit with people at lunch, even if you don't say much.

☀️ Keep a steady wake-up time.

Get up around the same time every day, and get some daylight in the morning. Sleep and mood are tightly linked. Running on Empty has more.

🗣 Tell one adult.

A counselor, a parent, a coach, a teacher. You don't need the perfect words. "I haven't felt like myself for a while" is enough.

🕵 Catch the story.

When your brain says "Nobody cares," ask: Is that a fact, or is that the depression talking?

✅ Count small wins.

Write down one thing you did each day, even tiny ones. Depression erases wins. A list keeps them.

Try itMake a menu for the hard days.

Write one tiny thing for each line. When you're stuck, pick one and do just that, for five minutes.

Five minutes counts. You can stop after, and lots of times you won't want to.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • What makes it hard for someone our age to say "I'm not okay"?
  • If a friend seemed off for a few weeks, what's one thing you could say to them?
  • Who at our school could a student go to if they were struggling?
Tip for the person leading

Assume some students in the room are dealing with depression right now. Don't ask anyone to share their own experience. Let students know a counselor is available afterward, and if anyone mentions self-harm or suicide, check in with them privately and follow your school's procedures.

Try itReal moments. What's your move?

Pick a move. There may be more than one good one.

It's Saturday. You've been in bed on your phone since you woke up. A friend texts, "Want to shoot hoops?" You don't feel like it.

You've felt down for weeks, and your grades are slipping. You're scared your parents will be mad.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make it heavier.
⏳ Waiting until you feel motivated.

With depression, motivation usually comes after action, not before.

🛏 Hiding in your room all day.

Rest is good. Hours alone with your thoughts usually make them darker.

📱 Endless scrolling.

It fills the time without filling you up, and it makes comparing yourself to others easy.

🙂 "Just think positive."

Depression isn't a bad attitude. Telling yourself to cheer up usually adds guilt.

🎭 Pretending you're fine.

Keeping it secret keeps the people who care about you from helping.

Get help right away if you or a friend

  • Has thoughts of dying, suicide, or hurting yourself.
  • Says things like "Everyone would be better off without me" or "I won't be a problem much longer."
  • Is giving away things they care about, or saying goodbye.
  • Feels completely hopeless or trapped.

Call or text 988 any time, or text HOME to 741741. Call 911 if someone is in danger right now. Tell a trusted adult today. See Get help below.

Talk to someone soon if

  • You've felt low, empty, or irritable most days for two weeks or more.
  • You've stopped doing things you used to like.
  • Sleep, eating, or schoolwork has really changed.
  • You're using vapes, weed, alcohol, or pills to cope.

Depression is very treatable. Therapy, and sometimes medicine, help most teens feel like themselves again. A counselor is a great first stop.
