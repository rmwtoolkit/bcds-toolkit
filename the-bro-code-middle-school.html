The Bro Code

Being a good friend and a real guy were never opposites.

What's going on

A lot of online content is built to find guys your age. It takes real wants, like being confident, strong and respected, and sells them back with a catch: respect comes from dominating people.

One video about getting stronger→The feed serves "alpha" creators→Anger keeps you watching→Someone jokes in the chat→Everyone laughs, you too→The line moves further

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "Am I allowed to say something?"
  • "It's just a joke."
  • "If I don't laugh, I'll lose friends."

🫀 Body

  • cringing inside while laughing
  • angrier at people you know
  • stung by "beta" or "soft"

🔁 In the group chat

  • laughing emoji to fit in
  • quoting an influencer like a coach
  • "gay" or "girly" as an insult

Part 1 · Understand it

What's really going on

Sound familiar?You didn't go looking for it. It found you.
  • You watched one video about getting stronger, and now your feed is full of guys yelling about "alpha" and "beta."
  • Someone in a game lobby or Discord said something about girls, everyone laughed, and you laughed too.
  • A friend started quoting an influencer like he's a life coach.
  • You've been called "beta," "simp," or "soft" for being nice to someone.
  • You've heard "gay" or "girly" used as an insult, and it landed on you or a friend.
  • A joke went too far and you weren't sure if you were allowed to say so.
  • You're a girl, and you've seen guys you know or like repeat stuff from these videos.

If any of this sounds familiar, you're far from alone. A lot of this content is built to find guys your age, whether you go looking for it or not.

📲 How it finds youAnger keeps you watching. That's the business.

Researchers have set up test accounts as teenage boys and watched what apps recommended. Even when the accounts only watched gym, gaming, or money videos, the apps quickly started serving creators who talk about girls with contempt.

It works because anger and shock keep people watching. Watch time is how apps and creators make money.

The worst part: it feels like you discovered it yourself. Really, it was delivered.

Why it pulls you inReal wants, sold back to you with a catch.
🎯 It starts with real stuff.
Wanting to be confident, strong, respected, and not lonely. Those are good things to want.
📦 Then it sells a package.
The package says respect comes from dominating people, and that girls are something to win, rate, or control.
🧾 Then it sells a product.
Courses, coaching, "secret" group chats, supplements. Usually with a monthly fee.
🔒 And it makes it hard to leave.
If you push back, you're "beta" or "brainwashed." That's a trick to keep you from thinking for yourself.

Three moves the content makes

Tap each one.

🏆Girls as points

Talking about girls like trophies, ratings, or a score that proves you're a real guy.

🎭Girls as the enemy

Saying girls are fake, manipulative, or only want money. It's a way of blaming a whole group for a guy feeling rejected.

🪜Guys as a ranking

Alpha, beta, sigma. It sorts every guy into a ladder, so friends become competition. Scientists who study wolves dropped the "alpha" idea years ago.

😂 "It's just a joke"Silence sounds like agreement.

Jokes are how a group figures out what's okay. Somebody tests a line. If everyone laughs, or nobody says anything, the line moves a little further next time.

Silence reads as agreement, even when you're cringing inside.

Here's what most people don't know: in studies, lots of guys say they're privately uncomfortable with this kind of talk, and wrongly guess their friends are fine with it.

What being a good guy actually looks likeConfident guys don't need to rank anyone.
💪 Strong
Strong enough to be the first one to say "nah," and to say when something hurts.
🤝 Loyal
Loyal to your friends, which includes not letting them become someone they'll regret.
😄 Funny
Funny without needing someone to be the punchline.
🧭 Confident
Confident enough that you don't need to rank anyone to feel okay.
❤️ Respectful
Treats girls like classmates, teammates, and friends, because they are.
🎨 Yourself
Quiet or loud, sporty or artsy, gay, straight, or still figuring it out. There's no one right way to be a guy.
Part 2 · Practice the skills

Staying yourself in the group

What actually helps

One low key "nah" goes a long way.

Tap one to see how.

🧠 Name the move.

When a video makes you angry at girls, ask: what does this person want me to feel, and what are they selling?

🎚 Keep the good, drop the package.

You can still lift, study, save money, and build discipline without signing up for the rest.

😐 The low key "nah."

You don't need a speech. "Bro, what," "that's weird," or a flat face often does more than a lecture.

🔀 Change the subject.

Moving the conversation is a real skill, and it works more often than people think.

💬 Talk one on one later.

"Hey, that stuff you said about her was kind of a lot." Friends hear it better without an audience.

🛡 Check on the person it was about.

A quick message like "that wasn't cool, you okay?" can matter a lot to them.

Try itThe chat just went there. What's your move?

You're in a chat with your friends. Pick what you'd actually do.

Someone posts a video of an influencer saying girls are "useless" with three laughing emojis. Everyone is reacting.

Your friend calls you "beta" for helping a girl in your class with homework.

You won't always get it perfect. One honest "nah" is more than most people say.

👥 In a groupPick a corner. Then see if your guess was right.

👥 For advisory or class · about 15 minutes · no materials needed

  1. The leader reads a statement. Students move or point to a corner of the room: agree, disagree, not sure, or it depends.
  2. Ask one or two people from different corners to explain why. No one has to share.
  3. After each one, the leader taps it to show what research says.
👥 Four corners · 1 of 4"Most guys at our school are fine with jokes that rate girls."

Agree, disagree, or not sure? Vote first.

What the research says

Usually wrong. Studies keep finding that guys guess their friends approve of this talk more than they actually do.

👥 Four corners · 2 of 4"Being called nice or respectful makes a guy less attractive."

Agree, disagree, or not sure? Vote first.

What the research says

Not what the research shows. In studies around the world, kindness is at or near the top of what people say they want in a partner.

👥 Four corners · 3 of 4"If you don't laugh, you'll lose your friends."

Agree, disagree, or not sure? Vote first.

What the research says

Sometimes it's awkward for a minute. More often, someone else was waiting for one person to go first.

👥 Four corners · 4 of 4"Alpha wolves prove there's a natural ranking of guys."

Agree, disagree, or not sure? Vote first.

What the research says

The wolf scientist who made "alpha" famous later said it was wrong. Wild wolf packs are mostly families.

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Why do you think so many guys go along with jokes they don't actually like?
  • What makes it hard to be the one who says "nah" in a group?
  • What do you wish online creators said about being a good guy?
Tip for the person leading

Keep it about content and situations, not named students. If someone gets defensive, that's okay: "Wanting confidence and respect makes total sense. The question is what gets you there."

What to do about your feed right nowYour feed learns from you. Teach it.
🙅 Tap "not interested."
Do it a few times in a row on these creators. The feed shifts quickly.
➕ Follow the real thing.
Athletes, coaches, and creators who talk about strength, discipline, or money without trashing anyone.
⏳ Watch the after-feeling.
If you feel angrier at people you actually know after watching, that's the content working on you.
👀 For girls, and anyone this stuff targets:
Muting and blocking is always okay. If a guy you know repeats this stuff to you, you don't have to debate him.

When it crosses a line

  • Someone is sharing photos of a real person, or threatening someone.
  • Talk about a girl turns into a plan to hurt, follow, or embarrass her.
  • You feel so angry or alone that these videos are the only place that gets you.
  • You have thoughts of hurting yourself or someone else.

Tell a counselor or a trusted adult today. You can also call or text 988, any time, day or night.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. A few of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

The Story of Ferdinand · Munro Leaf and Robert Lawson, 1936 Throwback

All the other bulls want to fight in Madrid. Ferdinand would rather sit under a cork tree and smell the flowers. There's no one right way to be a guy, and this book said so ninety years ago.

Hello, Universe · Erin Entrada Kelly, 2017

Quiet Virgil feels out of place in his sports-crazy family. Chet wishes the "weird kids" would stop being different, and his prank traps Virgil at the bottom of a well. Newbery Medal.

Heads up: Chet bullies Virgil and others.

🎬 Watch

How to Train Your Dragon · DreamWorks, 2010

Hiccup's whole village is built around killing dragons, and his dad expects him to be the toughest. He catches one and can't do it. Strong enough to be the first one to say "nah." Rated PG.

Cars · Pixar, 2006 Throwback

Lightning McQueen starts out caring only about winning. Remember the ending, when he stops short of the finish line to help the King? Rated G.

🎧 Listen

It's All Right to Cry · Rosey Grier, 1972

Sung by a former NFL defensive lineman on Free to Be... You and Me. Strong enough to say when something hurts.

🖼️ Look

The Cardboard Kingdom · Chad Sell and ten writers, 2018

A neighborhood of kids turns cardboard boxes into costumes and becomes knights, robots and monsters for a summer. Some of them are figuring out who they are along the way.
