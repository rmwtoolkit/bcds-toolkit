Green Flags, Red Flags

Watch how they treat you, not what they say.

What's going on

Green flags are respect, trust, honesty, and space to be yourself. Red flags are control, pressure, threats, and put-downs. Control can look a lot like caring, and it often starts small.

Lots of attention→"Where are you?" texts→Upset when you see friends→Checking your phone→It starts to feel normal

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "They're jealous because they care."
  • "I can fix them."
  • "I have nothing to hide."

📱 Online

  • demanding your passwords
  • angry at slow replies
  • checking your Snap Map

🫀 Feelings

  • a gut sense something's off
  • scared to disagree
  • feeling bad about yourself

Where to step in

Trust your gut. Keep your people.

  1. Know your green flags: they respect your no and are happy you have other friends.
  2. Keep your privacy. Trust doesn't require access to your phone.
  3. Talk to someone outside it. You can end any relationship that doesn't feel right.

Part 1 · Understand it

What healthy looks like

Sound familiar?Something feels a little off? Pay attention.
  • You have a crush, or someone has a crush on you, and you're not sure what a good relationship even looks like.
  • Someone wants your passwords, or to see your phone, "if you have nothing to hide."
  • Someone gets upset when you hang out with your friends without them.
  • Someone wants your location on all the time and asks why you went somewhere.
  • You've watched a friend change after getting into a relationship, and not in a good way.
  • You're not dating and not interested yet. That's completely normal too.

Lots of people your age aren't dating, and that's normal. Green and red flags show up in friendships and crushes too, so this is worth knowing either way. And the flags are the same whoever you like: a boy, a girl, or someone of any gender.

Healthy versus not

Tap each one.

💚Green flags

Respect, trust, honesty, and space to be yourself. You feel good about who you are around them.

🟡Yellow flags

Things that aren't okay but can sometimes be talked about and changed, like poor communication or occasional jealousy.

🚩Red flags

Control, pressure, threats, put-downs, or anything that makes you scared. These are not okay, ever.

Why it can be hard to tellControl can look a lot like caring.
Control can look like caring.
"I just want to know where you are because I love you" can sound sweet. Constant checking is about control, not love.
It often starts small.
Unhealthy relationships rarely start with big red flags. They tend to build slowly, so each step seems normal.
Movies and shows make it look romantic.
Jealousy, possessiveness, and big dramatic fights are often shown as signs of love. In real life, they're warning signs.
Phones make it easier to control someone.
Location sharing, read receipts, checking who you follow, demanding fast replies. Tech can turn a relationship into surveillance.
📱 Red flags onlineTrust doesn't mean handing over your phone.

Demanding passwords. Getting angry if you don't reply right away. Checking your Snap Map and asking why you were somewhere. Going through your followers or DMs. Pressuring you to post about them, or to delete people. Any of these can be a sign of control.

The worst part: a phone means control can follow you everywhere, even home, even to bed.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Trust your gut. Keep your people.

Tap one to see how.

💚 Know your green flags.

They respect your no. They're happy you have other friends. They're kind when they disagree. You can be yourself.

👂 Trust your gut.

If something feels off, it's worth paying attention to, even if you can't explain why yet.

👥 Keep your people.

Healthy relationships add to your life. They don't replace your friends, family, or activities.

🔐 Keep your privacy.

You're allowed to have your own passwords, your own phone, and your own space. Trust doesn't require access.

🗣 Talk to someone outside it.

A friend, family member, or counselor can help you see things more clearly.

🚪 Know you can leave.

You can end any relationship that doesn't feel right. You don't need a perfect reason.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese make it harder to see clearly.
🙈 Ignoring your gut.

That uneasy feeling is information.

🦸 Thinking you can fix them.

You can't change someone's controlling behavior by loving them more.

🙊 Keeping it secret.

Unhealthy relationships often depend on secrecy. Tell someone you trust. If you can't tell your family who you're dating, a counselor is a safe person to tell.

🔁 Excusing it.

"They're just jealous because they care." Caring doesn't look like control.

Get help right away if

  • Someone threatens you, hurts you, or makes you afraid.
  • Someone threatens to hurt themselves if you leave.
  • Someone pressures or forces you into anything physical or sexual.
  • Someone threatens to share private photos or messages, or to tell people who you like.

Other ways in

Some of the best red flags in movies belong to the villains. Some of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

Ella Enchanted · Gail Carson Levine, 1997

Ella was cursed at birth to obey any order anyone gives her. The whole book is about learning that she gets to say no. A Newbery Honor book.

🎬 Watch

Tangled · Disney, 2010 Throwback

Listen to Mother Gothel's song Mother Knows Best. Every line sounds like caring, and every line keeps Rapunzel in the tower. Control can look like caring. Rated PG.

Frozen · Disney, 2013 Throwback

Hans proposes the day he meets Anna, and she says yes. Elsa's line still holds: you can't marry a man you just met. Watch how fast he moves and what he turns out to want. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Speechless · Naomi Scott, 2019

Jasmine's song from the live-action Aladdin. Jafar tells her to stay quiet, and she won't. You're allowed to speak up too.

🖼️ Look

Real Friends · Shannon Hale, art by LeUyen Pham, 2017

Shannon's best friend joins The Group, where everyone competes to be Jen's favorite. Red flags show up in friendships too. A graphic memoir.

Heads up: Includes bullying, including from an older sister.

✏️ Make your own

Make two flag cards. On the green one, write three things the right person would do. On the red one, write the line from the Try it that you'd want to spot fastest. Keep them somewhere you'll see them.

  • The Trevor Project, for LGBTQ+ young people: call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678.
