Part 1 · Understand it

When someone asks for a photo

Sound familiar?You never owe anyone a picture.
  • Someone online you've never met in person asked for a picture of you.
  • Someone said "it'll disappear" or "I'll delete it right away."
  • Someone sent you a photo first, and now you feel like you owe them one.
  • A new online "friend" who says they're your age keeps asking for pictures.
  • You heard about someone at school whose photo got passed around.
  • You already sent something, and now you're worried.

Being asked for a photo can feel flattering, confusing, scary, or all three. You always get to say no, and if something already happened, there is help.

How the pressure works

Tap each one.

💬Flattery

"You're so pretty." "You're so cute." "I've never felt this way." Compliments that make it hard to say no.

⏳Persistence and guilt

Asking over and over, getting upset, or saying "if you liked me, you would."

🎣Trickery

Fake profiles pretending to be your age, sending a photo first, or promising it'll disappear.

What to knowOnce it's sent, it's out of your hands.
Once it's sent, you lose control of it.
Screenshots, screen recordings, and other phones mean a photo can be saved and shared, even on "disappearing" apps.
Sextortion is real.
Some people ask for photos, then threaten to share them unless you send more or pay money. This targets teens of all genders, especially boys, and it's a crime.
It's against the law.
Creating, asking for, having, or sharing sexual images of anyone under 18 is illegal, even if the person in the photo agreed. The person who pressured or shared is the one who did wrong.
It's never your fault if you're pressured, tricked, or threatened.
Adults are trained to help, and getting help is about protecting you, not punishing you. That's true whoever you are and whoever you like. If someone threatens to out you or tell your family, a counselor can help you figure out who to tell and how.
🛡 If you already sent somethingYou're not in trouble. Telling takes their power away.

You are not in trouble for being pressured or tricked. If someone is threatening you, do not pay and do not send more. Stop responding, save the messages, block, and tell a trusted adult right away. It's fixable, and you don't have to fix it alone.

The worst part: the person threatening you wants you to feel too ashamed to tell anyone. Telling is exactly what takes their power away.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

No is a complete answer.

Tap one to see how.

🛑 Say no, no explanation needed.

"No." "I don't do that." "Not happening." You don't owe anyone a picture.

😂 Use a deflection.

Some people send a funny meme, a picture of a pet, or a GIF instead. Humor can get you out of an awkward moment.

🚪 Notice how they react.

Someone who respects you will accept no. Someone who keeps pushing, guilting, or threatening is showing you who they are.

🔒 Block and report.

Every major app lets you block and report. Use it if someone keeps asking or makes you uncomfortable.

🧑‍🏫 Tell a trusted adult.

A parent or caregiver, counselor, teacher, or another adult you trust can help, especially if someone is threatening you.

🙅 Never forward someone else's photo.

If you receive a photo of someone else, don't save or share it. Tell an adult.

Try itPick the no you'd actually use.

Pick one that sounds like you, or write your own. Nothing is saved.

If something you sent is being shared or used against youDon't pay. Don't send more. Tell someone.
  1. Stop responding. Don't send more, and don't pay. Paying usually leads to more demands.
  2. Save evidence. Screenshot the messages and the account name before you block.
  3. Block and report the account on the app.
  4. Tell a trusted adult right away. You are not in trouble.
  5. Get the image taken down. NCMEC's free Take It Down service helps remove images of people under 18 from participating sites. Report threats to the NCMEC CyberTipline at 1-800-843-5678.
Try itThree real moments. What's your move?

Pick the best move.

Someone you met in a game chat says they're 13 too, and asks for a selfie in your pajamas.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make it worse, not better.
👻 Trusting "it'll disappear."

Screenshots and screen recordings exist.

💸 Paying or sending more.

It almost never makes threats stop.

🤐 Handling it alone out of shame.

The person threatening you is counting on silence.

🔁 Forwarding photos of others.

It hurts them and can get you in serious legal trouble.

Get help right away if

  • Someone is threatening to share a photo of you.
  • Someone is asking for money, more photos, or anything else in exchange for not sharing.
  • An adult is asking you for photos.
  • You feel hopeless or like you can't get out of it.

This is not your fault, and it can be fixed. Tell a trusted adult. NCMEC CyberTipline: 1-800-843-5678.

If you're thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988 now. LGBTQ+ young people can also reach The Trevor Project: call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678. See Get help below.
