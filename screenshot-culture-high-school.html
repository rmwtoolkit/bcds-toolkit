More, if you want it

Why copies travel

Disappearing messages and “close friends” control who sees the original. They can’t stop a screenshot, a screen recording, or a second phone. If each person shows three others, four rounds reach 121 people.

How common it is

In a 2022 Pew survey, 46% of US teens had been cyberbullied, and 7% had explicit images shared without consent. Most people who get exposed trusted someone. In 2017, Harvard pulled offers from at least ten incoming students over memes in a “private” group chat.

Also worth knowing

Sharing someone’s intimate images without consent is a crime, including AI fakes. Sexual images of anyone under 18 are illegal to create, keep, or share even when everyone agreed. Teens caught sexting may get an education program instead of prosecution. It’s still serious.

Skip these
  • “Disappearing means gone”The app deletes its copy. Every screenshot is a copy it can’t touch.
  • “It’s true, so it’s fair to share”A secret someone told you is still theirs.
  • Forwarding it to show how messed up it isThat’s one more copy. Check on the person and report it.
Other ways in

  • ReadMuch Ado About Nothing William Shakespeare, about 1598Claudio sees a woman he thinks is Hero at a window, out of context, and shames her in front of everyone at their wedding. He's wrong. Context collapse, four hundred years early.
  • WatchZootopia Disney, 2016 ThrowbackJudy says something careless about predators at a press conference. It spreads across the city, hurts Nick, and she has to own it. A good one for the person-in-it reader from the four-audience test. Rated PG.
  • WatchThe Social Network David Fincher, 2010Mark posts cruel things about his ex online, and she tells him, "The internet isn't written in pencil, Mark. It's written in ink." That's the you-in-five-years reader.Heads up: Rated PG-13 for sexual content, drug and alcohol use and language.
  • ListenEverybody Talks Neon Trees, 2011Tyler Glenn wrote it while he was closeted, about an ex who spread rumors that he was gay. A catchy song about how fast talk travels, and what it can put at risk.
  • LookThe Gossips Norman Rockwell, 1948A Saturday Evening Post cover: one rumor passes face to face down rows of neighbors, getting bigger, until it lands back on the woman who started it. Compare it to the 3, 9, 27, 81 diagram in Part 1.
  • MakeDraw your four-audience test as four faces reading the same post: your family, a stranger with power, the person in it, and you in five years. Give each one a speech bubble with their reaction.
