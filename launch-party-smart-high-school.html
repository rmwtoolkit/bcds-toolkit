What’s your move?

Maya’s roommate

First weekend. Maya finds her roommate on the bathroom floor, hard to wake, breathing slowly. Two friends say, “Put her to bed. Let her sleep it off.”

Those are CUPS signs. Call 911 or campus safety, roll her on her side, and stay. Not bed. Not coffee and a cold shower.

Theo

Doesn’t drink. At a party, people keep pushing cups at him.

Hold a seltzer, “I’m good,” and stick with his people. He doesn’t owe anyone his reasons.

Leo

Sees an older student refilling a first-year’s cup again and again, then steering her toward the stairs. She looks unsteady.

A distraction: “Hey, come get water with me.” Then find her friends. Waiting until tomorrow is too late.

Call 911 or campus safety right away if someone can't be woken, has slow or irregular breathing, has cold, clammy, or bluish skin, vomits while passed out, or has a seizure or chest pain. Blue lips or tiny pupils after a pill or powder: give Narcan too. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222, any time.

More, if you want it

The peak comes after the last drink

Early drinks are still being absorbed while you pour the next one, so blood alcohol keeps climbing after the last sip, even after someone passes out. Coffee, a cold shower or a greasy meal don’t speed it up. Only time does.

One drink is smaller than the cup

A standard drink is about 12 ounces of beer, 5 ounces of wine, or 1.5 ounces of liquor. People who free-pour overpour almost every time, and bigger cups mean bigger pours.

Pills, powders, edibles

About 2 in 5 fake pills tested held a potentially deadly dose of fentanyl. If it’s not from a pharmacy, you don’t know what’s in it. Edibles are slow, and taking more is the classic mistake.

If you choose to drink

Set your number sober. Eat first and have water between drinks. Pour your own and keep it with you. Skip pills and energy drinks.
