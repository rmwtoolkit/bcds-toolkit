Fake Pills

A familiar pill can still be fake.

What's going on

Fewer teens are using drugs, but more are dying, mostly from fentanyl hidden in fake pills. A tiny amount can stop someone's breathing, and you can't see, smell, or taste it.

Pills feel familiar→"It's from a pharmacy"→Actually pressed in a garage→Fentanyl you can't see→Breathing slows or stops

How you'll know

💭 What people say

  • "Pills are safer than street drugs."
  • "It's just a chill pill."
  • "It's probably a joke."

🆘 Overdose signs

  • won't wake up or respond
  • slow, shallow, or no breathing
  • blue or gray lips

📱 Where it shows up

  • "plugs" on social media
  • a group chat selling pills
  • a pill on the floor

Where to step in

Pharmacy, your name, or nothing.

  1. Only trust pills from a pharmacy with your name on the label.
  2. Know an overdose: won't wake up, slow breathing, gurgling, blue lips. Call 911 first.
  3. Naloxone (Narcan) reverses opioid overdoses. It can't hurt someone who isn't overdosing.

Call 911 right away if someone won't wake up, or can't talk. Give naloxone (Narcan) if you have it, and stay with them.

Part 1 · Understand it

Fake pills and fentanyl

Sound familiar?A familiar pill can still be fake.
  • You've heard kids joke about "chill pills" or "Percs."
  • Someone said pills are safer than street drugs because they come from a pharmacy.
  • You've seen something about fentanyl on the news and weren't sure what it is.
  • Someone said fentanyl is only a problem for people who use "hard drugs."
  • You've seen pills or "plugs" mentioned on social media.

Pills feel familiar. Most homes have some medicine around. That familiarity is exactly what makes fake pills so dangerous.

📉 Fewer teens are using. More are dying.Fewer teens using. More teens dying.

Teen drug use has gone down for years. But teen overdose deaths went way up, mostly because of fentanyl hidden in fake pills.

The worst part: many of them thought they were taking a regular prescription pill.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Looking out for each other

What actually helps

Pharmacy, your name, or nothing.

Tap one to see how.

🏥 Pharmacy, your name, or nothing.

The only pills you can trust come from a pharmacy with your name on the label. A friend's, a cousin's, or one ordered online could be anything.

👀 Know an opioid overdose.

Won't wake up or respond. Slow, shallow, or no breathing. Gurgling or snoring sounds. Blue or gray lips and fingertips. Tiny pupils.

📞 Call 911 first.

Then tell the 911 operator what happened and do what they say. Stay with the person.

👃 Naloxone (Narcan) reverses opioid overdoses.

It's a nasal spray. It's safe, and it can't hurt someone who isn't overdosing. Many pharmacies sell it without a prescription, and many health departments and community groups give it out free.

📚 Stress has better fixes.

When school or life feels like too much, a pill can sound like a shortcut. A counselor, sleep, and Study Drugs and Your Brain have better ones.

🧑‍🏫 Talk to someone.

Worried about a friend, or yourself? Counselors and the school nurse help with this all the time.

👥 In a groupSpot the fake. Then write the text.

👥 For advisory or class · about 15 minutes · no materials needed

  1. Go back to the 'Which one is real?' screen. Everyone votes: A, B, or can't tell.
  2. Reveal the answer, and give people a second to sit with it.
  3. In pairs, read this text from a friend: "my cousin can get us chill pills for the sleepover lol." Write back what you'd actually send.
  4. A few pairs share. The group picks the replies that sound most like a real friend, and least like a parent.
👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • What makes it hard to say something to a friend about this?
  • Why do you think so many people trust a pill more than other drugs?
  • Who's an adult at school you could go to if you were worried about a friend?
  • What would you want a friend to do if you were the one in trouble?
Tip for the person leading

Keep the replies about helping a friend, never about anyone's own experiences.

Try itReal moments. What's your move?

Pick a move. There may be more than one good one.

Someone in a group chat posts that they can sell "chill pills" to anyone who wants them.

You find a pill on the floor of the locker room.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

Call 911 right away if someone

  • Won't wake up, or can't talk.
  • Has slow, shallow, or no breathing, or is making gurgling or snoring sounds.
  • Has blue or gray lips or fingertips.
  • Took a pill that didn't come from a pharmacy and is acting very sleepy or strange.

Give naloxone (Narcan) if you have it, and stay with them. Worried about getting someone in trouble? Most states have laws that protect people who call for help in an overdose. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. See Get help below.
