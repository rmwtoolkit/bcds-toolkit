Lena

First in her family to go to college. Five weeks in, she’s skipping chemistry and eating once a day. On the Sunday call she says everything’s great. At home, you handle it yourself, or you pray about it.

She doesn’t have to choose between her family and help. The counseling center, a first-gen office, or a chaplain if faith is her way in. Then one email to her professor.

Wes and Sam

More, if you want it

Struggling is common

About 1 in 3 first-year students across 19 colleges screened positive for a mental health condition. Many students report high loneliness. These are screening surveys, not diagnoses. A rough start is common, and it’s usually a phase.

Help is opt-in

Campus counseling is often free or low-cost, and nobody signs you up. At 18, doctors and counselors generally can’t share your health information with your parents without your permission. A therapist in your home state may not be able to keep seeing you in another state. Families differ on what they expect to know, so talk it through before you go.

If a friend says there’s no point

Ask straight out: “Do you mean you’re thinking about killing yourself?” Asking doesn’t plant the idea. If yes or not sure: 988, campus safety, or an RA, that same night.
