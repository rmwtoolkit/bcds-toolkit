Liner notesSide A
A1

"Who am I?" is right on time.

Psychologist Erik Erikson called it the big task of the teen years. If you're wondering, you're doing it right.

A2

There are four places you might be.

Not looking yet"Haven't really thought about it."
Trying things on"Exploring. Not sure yet."
Handed to me"It's what my family or friends do."
This fits, for now"I looked around, and this is me."

You can be in a different spot for each part of you. Across 73 studies, the most common move was from trying things on to something that fits.

A3

You're made of a lot of pieces.

Personnel

  • race and ethnicity
  • family and culture
  • language
  • gender
  • faith
  • values
  • what you love
  • body and ability
  • money and class
  • online you

Some you were handed. Some you pick. Which ones are loudest right now?

Tucked in the sleeve

Gender and sexuality: your timeline, your words.

Some people feel settled early. For others, high school is when the questions get loud. You don't owe anyone a label or a coming-out. Who you tell, and when, is your call. The Trevor Project (below) is there any time.

A4

A different you in different rooms.

Most people switch how they talk and act depending on who's around. It's a skill. It gets heavy when there's nowhere you can be all of you.

A5

You'll keep changing.

In a study of 19,000 adults, people of every age said they'd changed a lot in ten years and expected to barely change in the next ten. They were wrong about the future. You don't have to decide everything this year.

Flip it over · Side B · Try one this week
  • B1
    Try something easy to undo.An elective, a club, a new genre, a different lunch table.
  • B2
    Interview your roots.Ask a grandparent or parent what they were like at your age, and what they hope you keep.
  • B3
    Find one place to be all of you.A friend, a team, an affinity group, a youth group.
  • B4
    Let people change.Give a friend room to reinvent. Ask for the same.

For teachers and the curious

Four corners (advisory, 15 minutes)

  1. "You should know who you are by graduation." (No. Identity keeps developing through your 20s.)
  2. "Most adults expect to change a lot in the next ten years." (They don't, and they do change.)
  3. "Feeling proud of your racial or ethnic background makes a difference." (Yes. Across 46 studies, it went with better self-esteem and fewer depression symptoms.)
