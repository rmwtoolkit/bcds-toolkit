Part 1 · Understand it

What's really going on in your feed

Sound familiar?You didn't search for it. It still showed up.
  • You watched a few videos about lifting or making money, and now your feed explains what women "really" want.
  • A guy in your grade quotes a creator about "high-value men" like it's settled science.
  • Someone in the chat rated girls from the junior class, and the only replies were laughing reactions.
  • You got called "soft" or a "simp" for texting a girl back like a normal person.
  • A friend has started talking about women as prizes or threats, and he seems angrier than he used to be.
  • You're a girl, and a guy you like has started repeating this stuff.

You probably already know this content exists. This page goes further: how it reaches you, why it works, and what the research says about the claims it makes.

How a feed drifts

In 2024, researchers at Dublin City University set up ten fresh phones with accounts posing as 16- and 18-year-old boys. Some searched only for gym, sports, and gaming. Every account was shown misogynistic or "alpha" content within 23 minutes.

It starts with real wantsConfidence, strength, money, respect, not being lonely. Those are good things to want.
Then it sells a packageRespect comes from dominating people, and women are something to win, rate, or control.
Then a productCourses, coaching, paid group chats, supplements. Usually monthly.
Then leaving costs youPush back and you're "beta" or "brainwashed." It's built to stop you thinking for yourself.
The claims vs. the researchAlpha wolves, "what women want," and other myths.
"Alpha" came from wolves in captivity.
Biologist L. David Mech, who helped make "alpha wolf" famous, studied wild wolves for 13 summers and concluded in 1999 that a typical pack is a family: two parents and their young. The old ranking picture came mostly from unrelated wolves housed together in captivity. He has since called the term misleading.
Kindness ranks high everywhere.
In a classic study of 10,047 people in 37 cultures, both men and women ranked "kind and understanding" and "intelligent" above earning power and good looks, in every sample. Looks and money mattered. They just weren't on top.
Most guys are less into it than they seem.
In a 2008 study, college men overestimated how sexist the other men in the room were. When researchers showed each group its real numbers, their estimates of other men's sexism were lower three weeks later. Thinking everyone else agrees is part of what keeps guys quiet.
The loneliness pieceIt sells belonging. Friends are the real version.

In a 2021 national survey, 15% of American men said they had no close friends, up from 3% in 1990. A lot of this content lands because it offers a team: guys who seem to get you, and someone to blame.

The habits that keep men connected as adults, like reaching out first, making plans, and talking about more than sports, get built now. That's a skill, not a weakness.

The worst part: it feels like you found it yourself. Really, it was delivered, and it profits from you staying angry and alone.

"It's just a joke"Silence sounds like agreement.

Jokes are how a group tests what's okay. Somebody pushes a line. If everyone laughs, or nobody says anything, the line moves a little further next time.

Silence reads as agreement, even when you're cringing inside, and the guy next to you may be cringing too.

What strong actually looks likeConfident guys don't need to rank anyone.
Strong
Strong enough to be the first one to say "nah."
Loyal
Loyal to your friends, which includes not letting them become someone they'll regret.
Funny
Funny without needing someone to be the punchline.
Confident
Secure enough that you don't need to rank anyone to feel okay.
Respectful
Treats women as full people, online and off, including when they aren't in the room.

What being a man means is shaped by family, culture, and faith, and people you respect may define it differently. Worth asking: which parts of what you were taught do you want to keep?

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Staying yourself in the group

What actually helps

One low-key "nah" goes a long way.

Tap each one.

1 Ask what it's selling.

When a video makes you angry at women, ask two questions: what does this person want me to feel, and what do they want me to buy?

2 Keep the good, drop the package.

Lift, study, save money, build discipline. None of that requires signing up for the rest.

3 The low-key "nah."

No speech needed. "Bro, what," "that's weird," or a flat look often does more than a lecture.

4 Change the subject.

Moving the conversation is a real skill, and it works more often than people think.

5 Talk one on one later.

"That stuff you said about her was a lot." Friends hear it better without an audience.

6 Check on the person it was about.

"That wasn't cool. You okay?" can matter a lot to them.

Try itThree moments. What's your move?

Pick what you'd actually do.

On the team bus, someone plays a clip of a creator saying women over 25 have "no value." Half the bus laughs.

Marco has been posting clips about how women "are all the same" since his breakup a month ago.

A friend calls you a simp for planning something for your girlfriend's birthday.

You won't always get it right. One honest "nah" is more than most people say.

In a groupPick a corner. Then see if your guess was right.

For advisory or class · about 15 minutes · no materials needed

  1. The leader reads a statement. Students move to or point at a corner: agree, disagree, not sure, or it depends.
  2. Ask one or two people from different corners to explain why. No one has to share.
  3. After each one, the leader taps to show what the research says.

Probably not. In studies of college men, guys consistently overestimated how sexist the other men around them were. When groups saw their real numbers, those estimates dropped.

What the research says

Four corners · 4 of 4"Alpha wolves prove there's a natural ranking of guys."

Agree, disagree, or not sure? Vote first.

What the research says

The wolf scientist who made "alpha" famous later said it was wrong. Wild packs are mostly families, led by the parents.

Talk about itThree questions for the group.
  • Why do so many guys go along with jokes they don't actually like?
  • What would make it easier to be the one who speaks up in our grade?
  • What did the men in your family or community teach you about being a man? What do you want to keep?
Tip for the person leading

Keep it about content and situations, not named students. If someone gets defensive, that's okay: "Wanting confidence and respect makes total sense. The question is what gets you there."

Your feed right nowYour feed learns from you. Teach it.
Tap "not interested."
Do it several times in a row on these creators. The feed shifts fast in both directions.
Follow the real thing.
Athletes, coaches, and creators who talk about strength, discipline, or money without trashing anyone.
Watch the after-feeling.
If you feel angrier at people you actually know after watching, that's the content working on you.
For girls:
Muting and blocking is always okay. If a guy you know repeats this stuff to you, you don't owe him a debate.

When it crosses a line

  • Someone is sharing photos of a real person, real or AI-made, or threatening someone.
  • Talk about a girl turns into a plan to hurt, follow, or embarrass her.
  • You feel so angry or alone that these videos are the only place that gets you.
  • You have thoughts of hurting yourself or someone else.

Tell a counselor or a trusted adult today. You can also call or text 988, any time, day or night. If someone is in danger right now, call 911.

Other ways in

Questions about what makes a man strong are old. These have better answers than your feed.

📖 Read

Of Boys and Men · Richard V. Reeves, 2022

Reeves takes guys' real struggles seriously, in school, work and friendship, and argues the problems are structural. It starts with real wants, without selling you someone to blame.

🎬 Watch

Won't You Be My Neighbor? · Morgan Neville, 2018 Throwback

The documentary about Fred Rogers. A friend in the film says his gentleness showed another way of being a man. Remember the sweater and the sneakers?

Heads up: Rated PG-13 for some thematic elements and language.

The Karate Kid · John G. Avildsen, 1984

The Cobra Kai sensei teaches aggression and tells his students to hurt Daniel. Mr. Miyagi teaches self-control. Two ideas of strong, side by side. Rated PG.

Heads up: Daniel gets beaten up by the Cobra Kai guys.

🎧 Listen

Boys Don't Cry · The Cure, 1979

A guy pretends he's fine after losing someone. Robert Smith said English boys were taught not to show emotion, and he pushed back on that.

I'll Make a Man Out of You · Donny Osmond, 1998 Throwback

The Mulan training song. Every recruit fails to climb the pole and get the arrow. Mulan figures it out. Listen again with "what strong actually looks like" in mind.

✏️ Make your own

Make a playlist of men who say what they feel. Five songs, any genre. Name it after the line from the lesson you most want to remember.
