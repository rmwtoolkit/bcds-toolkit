A Good Friend?

Some friendships charge you up. Some drain you.

What's going on

After time with this person, do you usually feel charged up or drained? Every friendship has off days. A pattern of feeling drained, nervous, or put down is worth noticing.

Super close one day→Ice cold the next→You wonder where you stand→You watch what you say→You start to shrink

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "Maybe I'm too sensitive."
  • "Maybe it's me."
  • "Better than being alone."

🫀 Feelings

  • drained after hanging out
  • nervous they'll get mad
  • bad about yourself

🔁 In the friendship

  • "just kidding" after something hurtful
  • "Don't sit with them"
  • you always apologize first

Where to step in

Change a friendship without ending it.

  1. Notice the pattern. One bad day isn't a pattern.
  2. Say something once, clearly: "When you joke about my grades, it bothers me. Can you stop?"
  3. Set a boundary: "If the jokes keep going, I'm going to leave." Then follow through.

Talk to an adult right away if a friend threatens to share your secrets, photos, or screenshots, or to hurt you, themselves, or someone else.

Part 1 · Understand it

Friendships that drain you

Sound familiar?Drained after hanging out? That's worth noticing.
  • You feel tired, anxious, or bad about yourself after hanging out with someone, and you're not sure why.
  • A friend makes "jokes" about you that don't feel like jokes.
  • You're careful about what you say so they won't get mad.
  • A friend says you can't be friends with someone else.
  • They're really fun sometimes and really mean other times, and you never know which you'll get.
  • You're always the one who reaches out, plans, and apologizes.

Noticing that a friendship drains you doesn't make you a bad friend. It means you're paying attention.

Three kinds of friendship that wear you down

Tap each one.

🎢The rollercoaster

Super close one day, ice cold the next. You spend a lot of energy trying to figure out where you stand.

🎯The put-down

Teasing that always seems to land on you, "just kidding" after something hurtful, or making fun of you in front of others. "Jokes" about your culture, your faith, your body, your family, or who you like count too.

🔒The controller

Telling you who you can sit with, getting mad when you have other friends, or using secrets to keep you in line, like threatening to tell people who you like.

Why it's hard to seeGood parts can hide the bad parts.
They're not all bad.
Draining friends often have great qualities too. The good parts make it confusing to admit the bad parts are a problem.
History is sticky.
If you've been friends since you were little, it can feel impossible to step back, even when the friendship has changed.
You might worry it's you.
If someone keeps telling you you're too sensitive, you can start to believe your feelings aren't reliable.
Losing them feels scary.
Even a hard friendship can feel better than being alone. That fear is real, and it's worth talking through.
🔋 The battery checkDo they charge you up or drain you?

A simple way to think about it: after time with this person, do you usually feel more charged or more drained? Every friendship has off days. A pattern of feeling drained is worth noticing.

The worst part: draining friendships can make you shrink. You get quieter, more careful, more worried. Good friendships help you feel more like yourself, not less.

📱 When it shows up on social mediaA controlling friend can follow you everywhere.

Draining friendships often show up online too:

  • They get mad if you don't reply right away.
  • They post things about you as "jokes" or tag you in embarrassing stuff.
  • They check who you're talking to or playing with.
  • They threaten to share screenshots or private messages.
  • They're nice in person and different online, or the other way around.

The worst part: online, a controlling friend can be in your pocket all day. It's hard to get a break from someone who's always there.

What actually helps

You can change a friendship without ending it.

Tap one to see how.

👀 Notice the pattern.

One bad day isn't a pattern. Feeling drained, nervous, or put down most of the time is.

🗣 Say something once, clearly.

Sometimes people don't realize how they're coming across. "When you joke about my grades in front of people, it really bothers me. Can you stop?"

📏 Set a boundary.

A boundary is what you will do, not what they have to do. "If the jokes keep going, I'm going to leave." Then follow through.

🔉 Turn the volume down.

You don't have to end a friendship to change it. You can spend less time together, share less, and put more energy into people who charge you up.

💬 Get an outside view.

A parent or caregiver, a counselor, or a friend outside the situation can help you see what's going on.

Try itReal moments. Okay, or not okay?

What do you think?

Your friend says, "If you sit with them at lunch, we're not friends anymore."

Your friend forgot your birthday, then felt really bad and made you a card the next day.

Every friendship has rough moments. Patterns matter more than single moments.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually make it worse.
🤐 Hoping it'll fix itself.

Sometimes it does. Usually, patterns keep going unless something changes.

🎭 Becoming who they want you to be.

Changing yourself to keep the peace can work for a while. It costs you a lot over time.

💣 Blowing it up publicly.

Calling them out in front of everyone usually turns into a big fight with sides. Stepping back quietly tends to go better.

🙈 Ignoring how you feel.

Your feelings are information. If something keeps feeling bad, take it seriously.

What to do about social media right nowMute, restrict, block. That's a boundary, not a war.
📍 Your location is yours.
You don't have to share it with anyone. Turning on Ghost Mode is not a betrayal.
⏱ You don't owe instant replies.
Good friends understand you have a life. Answering later is fine.
🧾 Save evidence.
If someone threatens to share your stuff, screenshot it and tell an adult.
🔇 Use mute, restrict, or block.
These tools exist for a reason. Using them is setting a boundary, not starting a war.

When it's more than a draining friendship

Talk to an adult right away if a friend:

  • Threatens to share your secrets, photos, or screenshots to control you.
  • Threatens to hurt you, themselves, or someone else.
  • Pressures you into things that are unsafe or that you don't want to do.
  • Gets others to gang up on you.

That's not a friendship problem you have to fix alone. See Get help below.

What this might mean

Feeling drained a lot is a signal worth listening to. It doesn't make you dramatic or mean.

What might help right now

  • 💛Make time for someone who charges you up.
  • 🗣Tell someone you trust what's been going on.

What this might mean

You can care about someone and still need things to change. Wanting a healthier friendship isn't betrayal.

What might help right now

  • ⚖️Look at the green and red flags above. What pattern do you see?
  • 📝Write down what you'd want to be different.
  • 🗣Talk it through with a counselor or trusted adult.

What this might mean

That fear makes sense, especially if this person is a big part of your social life. You don't have to make a big move all at once.

What might help right now

What this might mean

Most friendships are a mix. The question is whether the good outweighs the hard, most of the time.

What might help right now
