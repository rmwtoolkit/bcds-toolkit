Put It on the Calendar

A planning tool that talks each student through one assignment, one question at a time: what’s due, how long it’ll take, short sittings, where and when. It ends with each sitting on their calendar. The goal for the day is one real thing on each student’s calendar before they leave.

Open the student page

Before you start

Walk through the student page once yourself with a real assignment; it takes about three minutes. Ask students to bring one real assignment and to be signed in to their school Google account.

One way it can go

A suggestion. Change it to fit your group.

  1. Open with a question 2–3 min What’s the thing that always sneaks up on you? or Where did last Sunday go? Your own answer helps.
  2. Show them how 3 min Send the link. Put the page up on the screen and answer the first two or three questions together, out loud. If the page shows Add to my laptop, have everyone click it now, so the tool is one click away on a Sunday night.
  3. Plan one real thing 10 min Each student plans one real assignment and gets at least the first sitting onto their calendar. Walk around. When it asks where, “At school” lets them name lunch or E block. Quick finishers can tap Plan another one.
  4. Close 2–3 min Who put something on their calendar? When’s your first sitting?

Come back to it

One walk-through in September rarely sticks. A five-minute return at a crunch point does more: the first big test week, the stretch before midterms, the start of second semester. Did the calendar help? What did you have to move?

If it comes up

  • “I have nothing due.” Plan something real anyway: a practice, a work shift, college stuff, a long reading.
  • “I don’t use a calendar.” Fine. The plan and the “If it’s 4:00 and I’m at…” line work on paper too.
  • Calendar trouble. Students sign in with their Beaver Google account. If Calendar gives anyone trouble, the page also makes a calendar file for Apple or Outlook calendars, or they can copy the plan onto paper.
  • Practice, work, family, faith. Many students’ afternoons belong to someone else. The page tells them to plan around those first, so nobody has to explain their week.
  • Nothing is saved. The page keeps nothing and you can’t see what students enter. The only record is their own calendar.

If a student seems buried rather than busy (behind most days, not sleeping, panicky about it), a counselor should hear about it that day. Students with a learning plan or ADHD often get the most from this tool; there’s no need to single anyone out.
