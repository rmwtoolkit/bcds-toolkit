What Healthy Looks Like

What's going on

Most people know what they want to stop. Hardly anyone gets shown what to aim for. Healthy doesn't mean happy all the time. It's a range where you can feel something and still think.

Part 1 · Understand it

Picturing the trail

Sound familiar?You know what you want to stop. But what do you want instead?
  • You can list exactly what you want to stop: the arguing, the worrying, the 2 a.m. scrolling.
  • Someone asked what you want instead, and you weren't sure what to say.
  • Your friend group always has some kind of drama, and a calm week feels weird.
  • You think healthy people are happy all the time and never fight.
  • Saying no to someone made you feel selfish, even though it was the right call.
  • You've never really seen what a healthy friendship, or a healthy way to handle a bad day, looks like up close.

Most people know what they want to stop. Hardly anyone gets shown a picture of what to aim for. That's what this lesson is for.

🚵 Look where you want to goLook at the trail, not the rock.

Mountain bikers, skiers, and skateboarders all learn the same rule. Stare at the rock you're scared of and you'll hit it. Look at the line you want to ride, and you follow your eyes.

Life works a lot like that. If all you can picture is what you're trying to avoid, it's hard to steer anywhere else.

The good part: you can learn to picture the trail, even if nobody ever showed it to you.

What healthy is notHealthy doesn't mean happy all the time.
Myth: happy all the time.
Healthy people feel the full range: sad, mad, worried. And they come back.
Myth: no fights.
Healthy friends still argue. Then they repair it.
Myth: never needs help.
Healthy means knowing when to ask.
Myth: always calm.
Everyone gets knocked off balance. Healthy people recover a little sooner.
Myth: has it all figured out.
Healthy means staying curious and willing to change your mind.
Myth: there's one right way to look.
Healthy looks different across families, cultures, and faiths. Loud house or quiet one, two parents or a grandma raising you, whoever you are and whoever you like, there's a healthy that fits you.

Healthy is a range, not a spot.

Tap each one.

🔥Too much

Racing, flooded, snapping at people. Your body is on high alert.

↕️The window

You can feel it and still think. Psychiatrist Dan Siegel calls this your window of tolerance.

🧊Too little

Numb, flat, checked out. Your body hit the brakes.

What a healthy mind tends to doHealthy looks more ordinary than you'd think.
🎨 Feels the whole range.
Can name a feeling without being run by it.
🌿 Bends.
Changes plans or opinions when life calls for it. Psychologists call it flexibility.
🤝 Connects.
Can lean on people and still stand on their own.
🔧 Repairs.
Goes back after a rough moment and fixes what's fixable.
🧭 Lives by values.
Knows what matters to them, and lets that steer.
💛 Is kind inward.
Talks to themselves the way a good friend would.
What healthy friendships look likeHealthy friends still fight. Then they repair.
Fights stay about the issue.
Not about who you are as a person.
Hard things can be said.
Nobody is walking on eggshells.
Give and take.
Roughly even over time. Nobody's keeping score.
Closeness and freedom.
You can hang out a lot and still have other friends.
Someone goes back.
After a blowup, someone reaches out and repairs it, instead of going silent.

Boundaries: build a gate, not a wall.

Tap each one.

🧱A wall

Nobody gets in. It feels safe, and it gets lonely.

🚪A gate

You decide who comes in, and when. "Yes" and "not right now" both feel okay to say.

🌾No fence

Anyone walks through, any time. It's exhausting.

🏠 Why healthy can feel strangeStrange isn't the same as unsafe.

If you're used to a lot of drama, yelling, or silence, in your friend group, online, or at home, your brain learns that as normal. Then calm can feel boring. Kindness can feel suspicious. A good week can feel like the setup for a bad one.

Remember: strange isn't the same as unsafe. Often it means you're somewhere new.

📱 What feeds teach about healthyDrama gets clicks. That doesn't make it normal.

A lot of what you see online is a picture of healthy that nobody actually lives:

  • Accounts where everyone looks happy, calm, and put together all the time.
  • Relationship drama clips that make jealousy and fighting look normal, or even romantic.
  • Group chats where something is always going on, so calm feels boring.
  • Comment sections where being harsh gets the most likes.

The worst part: drama gets clicks, so your feed shows you a lot more rocks than trails.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Steering toward it

What actually helps

Small turns, long trail.

Tap one to see how.

🪨 Name the rock, then name the trail.

Write down what you want to stop. Then turn it into something you can do instead.

📅 Make your picture boring.

Skip "I'd be happy." Try "I'd eat breakfast before I check my phone." Ordinary details are easier to steer toward.

↪️ Take small turns.

Change is lots of tiny turns over a long distance. A big swerve tends to put you in the ditch.

👀 Borrow a picture.

Think of someone who handles hard moments in a way you respect: a coach, a grandparent, an aunt, an older cousin, someone from your faith community or neighborhood. Watch how they do it.

🧍 Stay with strange.

When calm feels weird, stay in it a little longer than feels comfortable.

🔁 Missed a turn? Turn again.

You don't have to start over. Look up, find the trail, and turn again.

Try itTurn "stop doing that" into "try this."

Most goals start as a rock, something you want to stop. Turn each one into a trail you can actually do this week.

"Stop fighting with my sister."
Walk away for five minutes when I feel myself getting loud.
"Stop worrying so much."
Do one thing today that worry tells me not to do.
"Stop being so hard on myself."
Say one thing to myself that I'd say to a friend.
Try itPicture a boring, good day. Then steer there.

Imagine an ordinary school day, three months from now, when things are going well. Pick one area and make it specific: body (sleep, food, moving however your body likes to move), people (who's there, how you talk), mind (your self-talk after a hard moment), or purpose (what you're proud of).

Try itFights, calm weeks, and saying no. What's your move?

Pick the healthy move.

You and a friend had a fight yesterday. It's awkward today and neither of you has said anything.

Your friend group has been calm for weeks. Part of you is bored and wants to stir something up.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese keep your eyes on the rock.
🎯 Aiming for perfect.

Nobody lives in the "happy all the time, never fights" column. Aiming there makes healthy feel impossible.

🪨 Staring at the rock.

Replaying what went wrong over and over keeps your eyes on exactly what you want to avoid.

💥 The big swerve.

Trying to change everything at once usually ends in the ditch. Small turns stick.

🌀 Going back to drama because it's familiar.

The old way has a pull. That doesn't mean it's good for you.

What to do about social media right nowYou don't owe anyone a reply at midnight.
🧭 Follow some trails.
Look for accounts that show ordinary, real life: hobbies, skills, people being kind.
🚪 Put a gate on your phone.
Do Not Disturb at night is a boundary. You don't owe anyone a reply at midnight.
🌀 Notice the drama pull.
If you keep opening a chat to see what's going on, ask whether it's adding anything.
🧘 Notice the after-feeling.
If an app leaves you feeling worse most times, that's information. Try a set time to check instead of all day.

If it gets heavy

  • You feel hopeless, or like nothing will ever get better.
  • Things at home or with a friend or partner feel unsafe.
  • You have thoughts of not wanting to be here.

Tell a counselor or a trusted adult right away. You can also call or text 988, any time, day or night.

Other ways in

Here are some pictures of the trail. A couple you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

The Rabbit Listened · Cori Doerrfeld, 2018 Throwback

Taylor's block tower crashes. Every animal has advice. Only the rabbit sits down and listens. That's what a healthy friend can look like.

The Vanderbeekers of 141st Street · Karina Yan Glaser, 2017

Five kids, one loud Harlem brownstone, and plenty of arguing. It's also a family where people show up for each other and fix things. Healthy doesn't have to be quiet.

Heads up: A neighbor is still grieving his wife and daughter, who died before the story starts.

🎬 Watch

Inside Out · Pixar, 2015

Joy tries to keep Sadness out of the way. It turns out Riley needs her. Healthy feels the whole range. Rated PG for mild thematic elements and some action.

Encanto · Disney, 2021

From the outside, the Madrigals look like they have it all figured out. Watch Luisa's song about the pressure she's under, and how Mirabel and her grandmother repair things. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Here Comes the Sun · The Beatles, 1969

George Harrison wrote it after a rough few months, on the first day he felt like himself again. Healthy people get knocked off balance too. They come back.

🖼️ Look

The Boy, the Mole, the Fox and the Horse · Charlie Mackesy, 2019

Ink drawings of four friends talking about being kind to yourself and asking for help. Flip through it and find the page that sounds like how you'd talk to a friend.
