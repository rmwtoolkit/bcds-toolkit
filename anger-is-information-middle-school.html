Anger Is Information

Anger is a signal, not a character flaw.

What's going on

Anger is your alarm saying "that's not okay." A line got crossed, something's unfair, or something hurts underneath. Your body gears up to fight, and the thinking part of your brain has less say.

A line gets crossed→The alarm goes off→Your face gets hot→Thinking brain loses its say→You bottle it or blow up

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "That's not okay."
  • "That's so unfair."
  • "Why am I this mad?"

🫀 Body

  • hot face, tight jaw
  • clenched fists
  • pounding heart

🔁 Behaviors

  • yelling, then feeling bad
  • typing while hot
  • bottling it until it explodes

Part 1 · Understand it

What anger is telling you

Sound familiar?Felt the heat rise? You're not alone.
  • Something small set you off and you weren't sure why.
  • You yelled at a sibling or parent and felt bad right after.
  • Someone was unfair to you or a friend and you could feel the heat rising.
  • You said something in anger that you can't take back.
  • You keep anger bottled up until it explodes.
  • People tell you to "calm down," which only makes it worse.

Anger is a normal, healthy emotion for everyone, whatever your gender, and whether your family says anger out loud or keeps it quiet. It's not bad to feel it. What matters is what you do with it.

What anger is trying to tell you

Tap each one.

🚧A line got crossed

Someone disrespected you, broke a promise, or ignored a boundary. Anger is your alarm saying "that's not okay."

⚖️Something's unfair

Anger often shows up when you or someone else is being treated unjustly, including for your race, religion, body, or who you are. That's worth paying attention to.

🧊Something's underneath

Anger is often the tip of an iceberg. Under it can be hurt, fear, embarrassment, sadness, or exhaustion.

Why it can take overHot face, pounding heart. Your brain hits the alarm.
Your body gears up to fight.
Anger triggers fight-or-flight: heart pounding, face hot, fists tight. In that state, the thinking part of your brain has less say.
Teen brains feel emotions strongly.
The emotional centers of your brain are very active during the teen years, while the brakes are still developing. Feelings can come on fast and big.
Stress, hunger, and tiredness lower your fuse.
You're much more likely to blow up when you're running on empty.
📱 Anger onlineScreenshots last longer than the anger.

Comment sections, group chats, and videos built to make people mad (sometimes called rage bait) can keep you angry for hours. And typing in anger is fast: it's easy to fire off something you'd never say in person.

The worst part: angry messages and posts don't disappear when you cool down. Screenshots last a lot longer than the feeling.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Catch it at a 4, not a 9.

Tap one to see how.

🌡 Notice the early signs.

Hot face, tight jaw, clenched fists, fast heartbeat. Catching anger at a 4 is much easier than at a 9.

⏸ Pause before you act.

Walk away, count to ten, get water. Say "I need a minute." Giving your body time to cool down gives your thinking brain time to catch up.

🐢 Cool it down.

A slow walk or roll, long slow breaths, cold water on your face, a stretch. Slow and calm helps anger fade. Punching a pillow or going all out keeps your body revved up and your mind on the fight. Save running and sports for regular days, when they help keep your fuse long.

🧊 Look under the iceberg.

Ask yourself: am I really angry, or also hurt? Embarrassed? Scared? Tired?

🗣 Say it, don't spray it.

Once you're calmer, say what's wrong: "I felt disrespected when you read my texts. I need you to ask first." That's assertive, not aggressive.

✍️ Write it out.

Write everything you're mad about, uncensored. Then decide what, if anything, you want to actually say.

Try itWhat was hiding under your last blowup?

Think about the last time you got really angry. Check anything else that might have been going on underneath.

Try itReal moments. Can you stand up without blowing up?

Which response stands up for you without making things worse?

Your little brother keeps going into your room and taking your stuff.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpBottling it or blowing up. Neither works.
🍾 Bottling it up.

Anger that's ignored tends to build and come out sideways, or all at once.

💥 Exploding at people.

Yelling, breaking things, or hurting someone causes new problems, even when your anger is justified.

📱 Typing while hot.

Angry texts and posts last forever. Wait until you've cooled off.

🔁 Replaying it over and over.

Some thinking helps. Constantly reliving it keeps you angry.

When anger is a sign you need more help

  • You feel angry most of the time, or it comes out of nowhere.
  • You've hurt someone, broken things, or hurt yourself when angry.
  • Anger is damaging friendships, family relationships, or school.
  • Someone else's anger makes you feel unsafe at home or school.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.

A reminder

Feeling angry doesn't make you a bad person. It means something matters to you. Learning to listen to anger, without letting it drive, is a real strength.

Other ways in

Anger shows up in a lot of good stories. Watch what's underneath it. A few of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

When Sophie Gets Angry, Really, Really Angry... · Molly Bang, 1999 Throwback

Sophie has to share her favorite toy and she explodes. She runs off by herself until she cools down, then comes home. A Caldecott Honor book.

Ghost · Jason Reynolds, 2016

Ghost is angry and running from a bad memory. Joining a track team gives the energy somewhere to go. A National Book Award finalist.

Heads up: Ghost and his mom had to run away from his dad, who drank and was violent.

🎬 Watch

Moana · Disney, 2016 Throwback

Te Kā is all fire and rage. Moana sees what's under the iceberg: Te Fiti, hurting because her heart was stolen. Rated PG.

Turning Red · Pixar, 2022

Whenever Mei's feelings run hot, she turns into a giant red panda. She learns to notice it early and calm it down. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

What Do You Do with the Mad That You Feel? · Fred Rogers, 1968 Throwback

Mister Rogers asked the question this whole lesson is about, and his answer was to do something that doesn't hurt anyone.

Let It Go · Idina Menzel, 2013

Elsa kept her feelings bottled up for years until they exploded. Saying it out loud is better than hiding it.

✏️ Make your own

Draw your anger iceberg. Put what people saw at your last blowup above the water. Below the water, draw or write what was really going on underneath.

What this might mean

Your body is in fight mode. That's not the time to make decisions or send messages.

What might help right now

  • ⏸Step away from the person or the phone.
  • 🚶Slow your body down: a slow walk or roll, a stretch, or cold water on your face.
  • 🌬Breathe in for 4, out for 6, until your heart slows down.

What this might mean

Hurt often hides behind anger because anger feels stronger. Both are real.

What might help right now

  • 🧊Try the iceberg check above.
  • 🗣Tell someone you trust what really hurt.
  • ✍️Write about the hurt part, not just the angry part.

What this might mean

Anger at unfairness can be fuel for speaking up. Channel it.

What might help right now

  • 📝Write down exactly what happened and what you want to change.
  • 🧑‍🏫Talk to an adult who can actually help fix it.
  • 🙋Speaking Up has ideas for standing up for yourself and others.

What this might mean

Being hard on yourself is a heavy kind of anger. Mistakes are how everyone learns.

What might help right now

  • 🫶Talk to yourself the way you'd talk to a friend who messed up.
  • 🧰If you hurt someone, The Repair Kit can help you make it right.
  • 🗯The Inner Critic can help with that harsh voice.

What this might mean

You found your way back down. Better days don't have to be perfect days to count.

What might help right now

  • 🔎Notice what helped you cool off.
  • 🧰Keep that strategy ready for next time.
  • 🎉Give yourself credit.
