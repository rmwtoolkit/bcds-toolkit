Some of the best friendships run on chirps. Who’s the chirp really for?

Think about it

The same chirp can bond or bury. What changes?

AnswerThe audience. Alone, it’s a joke between two friends. In front of the team, it’s a show.

Guess first

At 12 and 13, lots of boys have a best friend they tell everything. By 16 and 17, what happens to that friend?

AnswerMany said those friends were gone. A common reason: not wanting to seem gay or “soft.”

What’s your move?

The group chat

Jordan posts that his parents are splitting up, then adds “lol.” The chat starts roasting his haircut.

Keep the roast going and Jordan learns not to say real things there. A side text: “Hey, that’s a lot. You good?”

The bus home

After a tough loss, Sam says he worked really hard for this one. Someone says “bro’s glazing himself” and the bus laughs.

A quick “nah, he did.” Or a fist bump later. Either says plenty.

The text

You want to tell a friend you missed him this summer. You type it, add “no homo,” delete the whole thing, and stare at the screen.

“Missed you, man.” That’s enough. No disclaimer needed, and he’ll probably be glad.

More, if you want it

Bonds or buries?

Bonds: you both laugh, it’s about something you did, it stops on a bad day, you’d say it with nobody watching. Buries: one of you goes quiet, it’s about who you are, it gets louder with a crowd, it’s the only way you two talk.

The script

The writer bell hooks said boys are asked to cut off parts of themselves to be accepted as men. Your family, your culture and your faith shape how you show closeness. All of it counts.
