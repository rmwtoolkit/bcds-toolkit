Friendship Breakups

It can hurt as much as a breakup.

What's going on

A friendship can end with a slow fade, a blowup, or a drop. It's a real loss, even if nobody calls it one, and the sadness comes in waves.

Texts get shorter→Plans stop→They sit somewhere new→You see them every day→"What's wrong with me?"

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "What's wrong with me?"
  • "It shouldn't matter this much."
  • "They're fine without me."

🫀 Feelings

  • fine one week, wrecked the next
  • sad and relieved at once
  • angry and still missing them

📱 Online

  • checking their stories
  • removed from the group chat
  • old photos popping up

Where to step in

  1. Let it be sad. Cry, journal, or play the sad playlist.
  2. Tell someone the real version: a parent, cousin, counselor, or friend who isn't involved.
  3. Decide: try once to fix it, or let it end with kindness. Both are okay.

If you're having thoughts of hurting yourself, a counselor can help. See Get help below.

Part 1 · Understand it

When a friendship ends

Sound familiar?Losing a friend can hurt as much as a breakup.
  • A friend you used to talk to every day now barely says hi.
  • You and your best friend from elementary school ended up in different classes, and it just faded.
  • There was a big fight, and neither of you has talked since.
  • They told you, in words or by actions, that they don't want to be friends anymore.
  • They started sitting with a different group, and you weren't invited.
  • You were the one who stepped back, and you still feel sad or guilty about it.

Friendship breakups don't get talked about the way dating breakups do. They can hurt just as much, sometimes more.

The different ways it happens

Tap each one.

🌫The fade

No fight, no big moment. Texts get shorter, plans stop, and one day you realize it's over. Hard to grieve because there's nothing to point to.

💥The blowup

A fight, a betrayal, a line that got crossed. It ends fast, and it can leave anger and questions behind.

🚪The drop

One person ends it, clearly or by going cold. If it was you on the receiving end, it can feel like a rejection of who you are. If a friend pulled away after you shared something about yourself, like who you like or your gender, that says something about them, not about you. A counselor can help.

Why it hits so hardIt's a real loss, even if nobody calls it one.
It's a real loss.
You lost someone who knew your jokes, your history, your family, your secrets. Grief is the normal response to losing something that mattered, even when nobody died.
Nobody gives you a script for it.
There's no breakup song for friends, and people may say "just make new friends." Feeling like your sadness doesn't count makes it heavier.
You still see them.
Same hallways, same lunch period, maybe the same bus. Every day is a reminder.
It can shake how you see yourself.
You might wonder what's wrong with you. Friendships end for lots of reasons, and most of them aren't about one person being bad.
🌊 It comes in wavesFine one week, wrecked the next. That's normal.

Grief over a friendship doesn't go in a straight line. You might feel fine for a week, then see an old photo and feel it all over again. That's normal, not a step backward.

You're allowed to feel more than one thing: sad and relieved, angry and still missing them.

The worst part: watching them be fine, or seem fine, without you. Remember that you're seeing their outside, not their inside.

📱 When it happens on social mediaOnline, nobody really logs off.

A friendship breakup online can be confusing, because nobody really logs off:

  • They unfollowed you, removed you, or you're wondering if you should do it first.
  • They left the group chat, or you got removed from it.
  • Their posts with new friends keep showing up in your feed.
  • Old photos of the two of you pop up as memories.
  • Seeing that they viewed your story, and trying to figure out what it means.

The worst part: you can keep watching their life without you. Every check is a little reopening of the wound.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Let it be sad. Then decide what you want.

Tap one to see how.

💧 Let it be sad.

Stop telling yourself it shouldn't matter. Cry, journal, listen to the sad playlist. Feelings you let move through you pass faster than feelings you push down.

🗣 Tell someone the real version.

A parent or caregiver, a cousin, a counselor, a friend who isn't involved. Being listened to matters more than being advised right now.

🧭 Decide what you want.

Do you want to try to fix it, or let it go? Both are okay. If you want to try, see The Repair Kit. If not, you can let it end with kindness.

🔕 Give yourself distance online.

Mute, unfollow, or archive old chats for a while. Not to be dramatic, just so you're not getting hit every time you open your phone.

🌱 Make some room for new.

Not replacing them. Just saying yes to one new thing: a club, a team, sitting with someone different at lunch.

Try itReal moments. Would you fix it, or let it go?

Read each one. What would you lean toward?

Your friend has been sitting with a new group for a month. When you say hi, they're polite but distant. You miss them a lot.

You and a friend had a big fight about something they said behind your back. They apologized and seem to mean it.

There's no single right answer. What matters is that you choose, instead of getting stuck.

What doesn't actually helpThese keep you stuck.
🔁 Replaying it over and over.

Going back through old messages or rerunning the fight in your head. Some thinking helps you understand. Constant replaying keeps the wound open.

🗡 Trashing them to everyone.

Venting to one trusted person is healthy. Telling everyone what a terrible person they are tends to spread, and come back around.

📸 Checking their stories.

Watching their life without you is a fresh hurt every time. Mute is allowed.

🙈 Pretending you're totally fine.

You don't have to fall apart in public. You also don't have to act like nothing happened when something did.

Try itWrite it all down. It goes nowhere.

Write what you wish you could say to them. All of it. It stays on this page and goes nowhere. Writing it out can help you figure out what you actually feel, and whether there's anything you want to say for real.

When you're done, read it back. Is there one sentence you'd actually want them to hear? That's the one to think about saying, calmly, one on one. Or none, and that's okay too.

What to do about social media right nowMute first. Decide the rest later.
🔕 Mute before you unfollow.
Muting is private and reversible. You can decide about unfollowing later, when it's less raw.
🗂 Archive old chats and photos.
Not deleted, just out of sight for now.
🙅 Skip the subtle posts.
Posting things meant for them to see keeps you tied to them. Post for yourself, or not at all.
👀 Stop decoding views.
Who watched your story tells you almost nothing. Try not to read into it.

When to get more help

  • You've been sad most days for more than two weeks, or you've stopped enjoying things you used to like.
  • You're not sleeping or eating normally.
  • The ex-friend is spreading things about you or turning people against you.
  • You're having thoughts of hurting yourself.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Losing a friend is old news in stories. A few of these you might remember from when you were little, and they hit different now.

📖 Read

The House at Pooh Corner · A.A. Milne, 1928 Throwback

At the very end, Christopher Robin is growing up and going away. Nobody is mad. It's a fade, and Milne lets it be sad and loving at the same time.

Shouting at the Rain · Lynda Mullaly Hunt, 2019

Every summer Delsie's best friend Brandy comes to Cape Cod. This year Brandy has outgrown her and has a new friend who's mean to Delsie. Then Delsie meets Ronan.

🎬 Watch

The Fox and the Hound · Disney, 1981 Throwback

Tod and Copper are best friends as babies. As they grow up, the people around them pull them apart. Rated G.

Heads up: There's a scary bear fight, and a hunting dog gets badly hurt.

🎧 Listen

When She Loved Me · Sarah McLachlan (written by Randy Newman), 1999 Throwback

Jessie's song from Toy Story 2. Her girl grew up and slowly stopped playing with her. No fight, just the fade, and it still hurts.

🖼️ Look

Real Friends · Shannon Hale and LeUyen Pham, 2017

Shannon's best friend Adrienne starts spending time with the popular group, and Shannon has to figure out where she fits. A graphic memoir of the author's own childhood.

Heads up: Shannon's older sister is often mean to her at home, and Shannon struggles with worry.

What this might mean

You're grieving something real. Missing someone doesn't mean you should go back. It means it mattered.

What might help right now

  • 💧Let yourself be sad for a bit. Put on music, cry if you need to.
  • 🗣Tell someone the real version, someone who'll just listen.
  • 📷Put old photos and chats somewhere you won't see them by accident for a while.
  • 🫶Do one thing that feels like you: a hobby, a walk, a show you love.

What this might mean

Anger usually shows up when something crossed a line. It's worth listening to, and worth not acting on in the heat of it.

What might help right now

  • 📝Write the unsent letter above. Say all of it there.
  • 🏃Move your body: a run, a walk or roll, loud music.
  • ⏳Wait until you're calmer before talking to them or about them.
  • 🙅Skip the vague posts. They keep you tied to it.

What this might mean

Friendships usually end for more than one reason, and rarely because of one person. You can own your part without carrying all of it.

What might help right now

  • ⚖️Write down what was yours, what was theirs, and what was just life.
  • 🧰If you want to apologize for your part, The Repair Kit can help.
  • 🗣Ask someone you trust what they think. Guilt tends to exaggerate.

What this might mean

When one friendship was a big part of your day, there's a hole where it used to be. That gap is temporary, even though it doesn't feel like it.

What might help right now

  • 💬Text one person you've drifted from: "hey, been a while, how are you?"
  • 🎯Pick one place to show up this week: a club, practice, a study group.
  • 🧭Finding Your People has ideas for building new friendships.
  • 🗣Tell a counselor. Helping students find their people is part of their job.

What this might mean

Some distance is helping. Better days don't have to be perfect days to count.

What might help right now

  • 🔎Notice what helped today, so you can do it again.
  • 💛Spend time with people who feel easy to be around.
  • 🌱Let yourself enjoy things without feeling guilty about it.
