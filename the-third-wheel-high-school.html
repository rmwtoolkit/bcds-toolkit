Accident, drift or on purpose?

Nadia

Started dating someone in October. She used to drive you home after practice. Now she drives them. She still texts you most nights.

Ask for protected time: “Can we keep Thursdays?” A new relationship often costs a couple of close friends. It’s still a real loss.

Ryan

Posts a story from a late study session with three friends. Earlier that week, you told him you work Tuesday nights.

He probably assumed you were working. Try: “Next time ask me anyway. I’m free most other nights.”

The lunch group

For three weekends, plans happen in a separate chat. When you walk up, the talk stops. “It’s nothing.”

A pattern, plus the talk stopping. One honest sentence to the one you trust most there, and more time with people who make room. If it’s targeted, tell an adult.

More, if you want it

Why small stuff hits hard

In one study, 1,486 people in 62 countries played an online ball toss. When the other two stopped throwing to them, they felt worse, even though the other two were a computer.

What backfires

Disappearing to see who notices. Keeping score. Making people choose. Replaying their stories.

Some people miss more plans

Curfews, faith commitments, family responsibilities or a job can mean missing more plans. That’s logistics, not a verdict. Say what works so friends keep asking.

Other ways in

Somebody is always the odd number in a story. One of these goes back to elementary school.

  • ReadDiary of a Wimpy Kid: The Third Wheel Jeff Kinney, 2012 ThrowbackGreg and Rowley head to the Valentine's dance as part of a group, and somebody is going to end up the odd one out. Reread it now that you know the four needs.
  • ReadSince You've Been Gone Morgan Matson, 2014Emily always lived in her best friend Sloane's shadow. When Sloane disappears, she leaves a list, and Emily has to start making plans and friends on her own.
  • WatchFerris Bueller's Day Off John Hughes, 1986Cameron spends the whole day tagging along with Ferris and his girlfriend. Near the end he decides he's done letting things happen to him. That's control coming back.Heads up: Rated PG-13 for language.
  • ListenDancing On My Own Robyn, 2010She's in the corner of the club, watching. Physically there, and still outside.
  • LookNighthawks Edward Hopper, 1942Four people in a late-night diner, all in the same room and none of them really together. Notice the one sitting off by himself.
  • MakeWrite the four needs from the lesson on a card: belonging, self-esteem, control, mattering. Next time you feel like the extra one, check the one that took the hit, then write one plan you could make yourself.
