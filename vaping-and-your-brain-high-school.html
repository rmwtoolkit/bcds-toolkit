Vaping relieves stress. Myth or fact?

What would you say or do?

Lucas

Hits his vape between every class. Halfway through a two-hour practice SAT he’s shaky, can’t focus, and walks out early.

Shaky and foggy without it are signs of dependence. A quit plan beats sneaking a hit at the break.

Nia

Switched from vaping to nicotine pouches. “It’s not vaping,” she says, “so I quit.”

Easier on the lungs. Still nicotine. Same loop.

A parent

Smokes. Started vaping to cut down, and says it’s “basically harmless.”

For an adult smoker who switches all the way, it can cut harm. For a teen who doesn’t smoke, it only adds nicotine. Same product, different decision.

More, if you want it

Myth or fact

Pouches aren’t addictive. Myth. Same nicotine, same hook.
Hooked within weeks. Fact. About 1 in 5 occasional users showed a sign within four weeks.
“Nicotine free” means none. Myth. Some tested positive, and labels on these aren’t checked.

Why teen brains get hooked faster

Nicotine sets off dopamine in seconds and tags the hit, and everything around it, as worth repeating. The parts of your brain for focus and impulse control keep building into your mid-20s.

Vapes and cigarettes

For an adult smoker who switches all the way, vaping can cut harm. For a teen who doesn’t smoke, it only adds nicotine. Same product, different decision.
