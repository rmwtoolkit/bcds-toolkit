Part 1 · Understand it

How the feed hooks you

Sound familiar?Opened it for one thing. Looked up an hour later.
  • You opened an app to check one thing and looked up an hour later.
  • You reached for your phone without even deciding to.
  • You kept scrolling even though you weren't enjoying it anymore.
  • You stayed up late watching videos and were wrecked the next day.
  • You saw scary or upsetting news and couldn't stop reading more.
  • You felt anxious or empty after a long scroll, but couldn't say why.

If it feels hard to put your phone down, that's not a willpower problem. Apps are designed by experts to keep you scrolling.

How the feed keeps you hooked

Tap each one.

♾No stopping points

Infinite scroll and autoplay remove the natural end of a page or episode. There's never a moment that says "you're done."

🎰Surprise rewards

You never know if the next video will be amazing, so you keep swiping. Slot machines use the same trick.

🎯Made for you

Algorithms learn exactly what keeps you watching, including stuff that makes you anxious or angry.

Why it takes more than it givesThose hours come from somewhere.
It steals time from things that help.
Sleep, friends, homework, hobbies, moving your body. Hours of scrolling come from somewhere.
Bad news grabs attention.
Brains are wired to notice threats. Scary or upsetting content can pull you in and leave you more anxious. It can hit even harder when the news is about people who share your race, religion, home country, or identity.
It leaves you drained.
Lots of people feel tired, flat, or restless after a long scroll, even though it felt relaxing while it was happening.
It's not all bad.
Connecting with friends, learning, laughing, finding your people. For lots of kids, including LGBTQ+ kids and kids who feel different at school, online is where they find people like them. Phones can do a lot of good. The goal is choosing how you use it.
📱 The scroll and your moodHow you scroll matters more than how long.

Research on teens and social media is mixed. How you use it matters more than the hours alone. Actively connecting with friends tends to feel better than passively scrolling. Using it late at night, or to avoid feelings, tends to feel worse.

The worst part: when you feel down, scrolling feels like comfort. Then it often leaves you feeling more down, and the cycle repeats.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

Change your setup, not just your willpower.

Tap one to see how.

🛑 Decide before you open.

Before opening an app, decide what you're there for and how long. Set a timer if you need one.

🧭 Notice the reach.

Catch the moment you reach for your phone. Ask: what am I feeling right now? Bored? Anxious? Avoiding something?

🏠 Move apps off your home screen.

Put the most time-sucking apps in a folder on a back page. A few extra taps break the autopilot.

🌙 Phone-free zones.

Bed, meals, homework time. Pick one and keep the phone out of it.

⏱ Use screen time tools.

Most phones let you set daily limits for specific apps. They're easy to override, but the reminder helps.

🧩 Have a replacement.

Know what you'll do instead: music, a book, a walk, drawing, texting one friend.

Try itNext time you grab your phone, ask why.

Next time you grab your phone without thinking, pause and pick what's going on. Notice patterns.

Try itLate nights, bad news. What's your move?

Pick the best move.

It's 9:45 on a school night and you're on your 30th video.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese sound good but rarely stick.
💪 Relying on willpower.

You're up against teams of designers. Change your setup, not just your intentions.

😔 Shaming yourself.

Feeling bad about screen time can make you want to escape into your phone more.

🛏 Phone in bed.

It's the hardest place to stop, and it costs you sleep.

🚫 Going cold turkey without a plan.

If you take something away, put something in its place.

When to get more help

  • You can't cut back even when you really want to.
  • Phone use is hurting your sleep, grades, or relationships.
  • You're using your phone to escape feelings that seem too big to handle.
  • Something you saw online is upsetting you and won't go away.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.
