Texted at random, how often were people’s minds somewhere else?

AnswerAbout half. 47% of the time. And people felt less happy while their mind wandered, even during nice daydreams.

What it is

Paying attention to right now, on purpose, without judging it.

Nobody empties their mind. The skill is noticing you drifted and coming back, kindly.

coming back is the practice

One rep

Focus, wander, notice, come back.

Every time you notice and come back, that’s one rep. Scolding yourself makes the puppy run further.

A month, five minutes a day. Which one lifted mood more?

AnswerThe exhales. Long, slow breaths out beat meditation for mood. Both helped. Your out-breath is a built-in brake.

Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023

More, if you want it

The roots

Buddhist sati and loving-kindness are the roots, and Jon Kabat-Zinn’s secular course (1979) brought it into hospitals. Centering prayer, dhikr, the rosary and many other traditions also practice coming back. No beliefs are required.

What the research shows

Minds wander about half the time. Naming a feeling turns the brain’s alarm down. In schools, a big study found mindfulness classes worked about as well as usual lessons, mostly because students stopped practicing. It helps the people who use it.

Every body counts

Eyes open is fine. Moving counts. Prayer counts. If breath focus makes you more anxious, use sounds, your hands or your feet as the anchor instead.
