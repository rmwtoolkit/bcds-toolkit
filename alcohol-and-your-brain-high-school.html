First, the real number

76 out of 100 tenth graders didn’t drink at all last year.

For seniors it’s 59 out of 100. Not drinking is the normal choice.

What goes first

Judgment goes first.

Then memory, then balance, then breathing. The part that would have noticed is the first to go.

When to call

Any one of these: call 911.

Can’t be woken. Throwing up while passed out. Slow or gappy breathing. Cold, pale or bluish skin, or a seizure. On their side, top knee bent. Stay with them.

Call your exit-plan person, and offer Jay a ride too. Under 21, even a little alcohol can cost a license.

Ava

Bring it up later, privately, when she’s sober. Three blackouts is a pattern worth a counselor’s help.

More, if you want it

The last drink isn’t the peak

Alcohol keeps moving into the blood for 30 to 90 minutes after the last sip, and only time brings it down. Coffee, a cold shower or throwing up don’t speed it up. Someone fine at 10:30 can be in danger at 11:15.

Teen brains get fewer stop signals

Teens feel less of the sleepy, wobbly warning that tells adults they’ve had enough. People who started before 14 were about five times as likely to become dependent as people who waited until 21.

Wherever it comes from, it hits the same

A parent’s cabinet, an older friend, a sleepover. Where it came from doesn’t change what it does in your body.
