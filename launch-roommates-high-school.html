Sofia and Hannah

Sofia calls home every night at 10, on speaker, with her abuela and little brothers. Hannah goes to bed at 10:30 and has started sighing and putting her headphones on hard. Sofia thinks Hannah doesn’t like her.

Both are avoiding. Both are guessing. Hannah: “I love that your family is so close. The volume at 10 is hard for me. Could we figure something out?” Sofia can open it too.

Dev and Luke

Luke hasn’t left the room in five days, isn’t showering, and stopped going to class. Dev overheard him on the phone saying he “doesn’t see the point.” Telling the RA feels like going behind his back.

This is a safety situation, not a roommate problem. Ask straight out: “Are you thinking about hurting yourself?” Tell the RA today. Telling isn’t betrayal.

More, if you want it

Your roommate rubs off on you

When a college assigned first-year roommates at random, students’ grades moved with their roommates’ grades. Sleep, noise and habits rub off. The room is worth some effort.

Start soft and specific

“When the lights are on after 1 a.m., I can’t fall asleep, and I have an 8 a.m. Could we try 12:30 on weeknights, with a desk lamp?” What you notice, how it affects you, what you’re asking.

Different isn’t a problem to fix

In one study of random roommates across race, early discomfort faded, and by the end of the term people were more comfortable with difference. Give it a semester. Comments that target who someone is aren’t a style difference: tell the RA.

Other ways in

Sharing a room with someone new is an old story. One of these you might remember from when you were little.

  • ReadRoomies Sara Zarr and Tara Altebrando, 2013EB from New Jersey and Lauren from San Francisco are assigned to share a room at Berkeley and spend the summer emailing. It starts with the mini-fridge and gets real fast. Two authors, one writing each girl.
  • ReadFangirl Rainbow Rowell, 2013Cath and her roommate Reagan could not be more different, and Reagan is blunt about it. Somewhere along the way, they figure each other out.Heads up: Frequent strong language, some drinking, and a father's bipolar episode. Common Sense Media says 14+.
  • WatchMonsters University Pixar, 2013Mike and Sulley start as rivals, all competing. After they lose everything, they start collaborating, and that's how they get out. Rated G.
  • WatchBert and Ernie Sesame Street, 1969 ThrowbackThey share an apartment and a bedroom. Bert likes things neat and quiet, and Ernie doesn't. Watch a few old sketches and name Bert's conflict style.
  • ListenEbony and Ivory Paul McCartney and Stevie Wonder, 1982Black and white piano keys, side by side, making it work. Goes nicely with the Shook and Fazio study on roommates from different backgrounds.
  • MakeDraw the room from the Rosa and Hannah story. On each side, write what that person is assuming. Then write the notice, effect, ask sentence in a speech bubble between them.
