Guess first

Someone over an intercom seems to have a seizure. How many go for help if they think they’re alone? If four others can hear?

Answer85% when alone. 31% with four others. Everyone thinks someone else has it.

Darley and Latané, 1968

Kids were around for most bullying. How often did one step in?

AnswerAbout 19% of the time. When they did, it stopped 57% of the time. The people watching hold a lot of power.

Which move would you actually make?

Locker room

A teammate makes a comment about another player’s body. A few guys laugh. The player goes quiet.

“Bro, chill,” and change the subject. Or check on him after: “That was out of line. You good?”

At a party

Someone keeps following Ana from your chem class. She looks uncomfortable and keeps scanning the room.

Walk over: “Hey, we’re heading to the kitchen, come with us.” Or get a couple of friends or the host to step in together.

The class chat

Someone posts a voice clip mocking Leo’s accent. In a 30-person chat, reactions start rolling in.

Screenshot it for an adult and share it nowhere else. Check on Leo. “Not okay, delete it,” if that feels safe.

More, if you want it

Five gates

Between seeing it and doing something: notice it, call it a problem, feel it’s yours, know a move, act. Most people stall at feeling it’s theirs and knowing a move.

Friends act more

In 219 real public fights caught on camera, someone helped in 9 of 10, and more people around made help more likely. Friends can look at each other and check.

Other ways in

Every one of these has someone standing at one of the five gates, deciding.

  • ReadStargirl Jerry Spinelli, 2000When the whole school turns on Stargirl, Leo is one of the few who stays. Then he feels the cost and asks her to act more normal. Gate 5 up close: knowing what's right and being scared of being next.
  • ReadThe Lorax Dr. Seuss, 1971 Throwback"Unless someone like you cares a whole awful lot, nothing is going to get better." That's gate 3, taking it on yourself, in a picture book you probably had at five.
  • WatchRemember the Titans Boaz Yakin, 2000A white player won't block for Rev, a Black teammate, and he ends up cut from the team. Watch how the norm on that team gets set by the players, not only the coaches.Heads up: Rated PG for thematic elements and some language. Includes racial slurs, a brick thrown through a family's window, and a car crash that leaves a player paralyzed.
  • ListenWhere Is the Love? Black Eyed Peas, 2003The whole song asks why so many people see what's wrong and keep walking. Listen for the line about having love only for your own race.
  • LookMarch: Book One John Lewis, Andrew Aydin and Nate Powell, 2013Before the Nashville lunch counter sit-ins, students trained together and planned what they would say and do. Gate 4, know what to do, worked out ahead of time and in a group.Heads up: Shows racist violence against civil rights protesters.
