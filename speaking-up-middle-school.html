Speaking Up

One person can change how the moment goes.

What's going on

Most people want to help when they see something wrong. It's hard because everyone waits for someone else to go first, and you might worry you'll be next. Knowing how makes it easier.

Someone gets picked on→Everyone is laughing→"It's just a joke"→"Someone else will"→You stay quiet

How you'll know

💭 Thoughts

  • "It's just a joke."
  • "What if I'm next?"
  • "I don't know what to say."

🫀 Body

  • you freeze
  • you laugh along
  • scary and good at once

📱 In the group chat

  • a mean post about someone
  • laughing emojis piling up
  • photos of a hangout they missed

Where to step in

You don't need a speech. One move.

  1. Have your line ready: "That's not funny." "Come on, let's go."
  2. Distract or include: invite the person away, wait for them, save them a seat.
  3. Delay or delegate: check in afterward, or tell an adult. That's not snitching.

Get an adult right away if someone is being physically hurt or threatened.

Part 1 · Understand it

Why speaking up matters, and why it's hard

Sound familiar?You saw it. You froze. That's normal.
  • You saw someone get teased, and you laughed along or stayed quiet.
  • Someone in the group chat posted something mean about a kid who isn't in it.
  • You noticed a kid on the edge of the group, left behind when everyone got up and walked off together.
  • Someone said something racist, sexist, homophobic, or transphobic, or mocked someone's religion, disability, or accent, and it was played off as a joke.
  • You wanted to say something but didn't know what, or worried you'd be next.
  • You did say something, and it felt scary and good at the same time.

Most people want to help when they see something wrong. Knowing how makes it a lot easier.

Being an upstander

Tap each one.

👀Notice

Pay attention to who's being left out, picked on, or talked about, in person or online.

🧠Decide it matters

It's easy to think "it's just a joke" or "someone else will do something." Decide that it's worth acting on.

🤝Do one thing

It doesn't have to be dramatic. One small action can change how the moment goes.

Why speaking up is hardEveryone's waiting for someone else to go first.
The bystander effect.
Research shows that the more people who see something, the less likely any one person is to help. Everyone assumes someone else will.
Fear of being next.
It's a real worry, especially if you've been picked on for who you are too. Many of the ways to help don't put you in the spotlight.
It's not clear it's serious.
Teasing between friends and cruelty can look similar. Watch the person's face. If they don't look like they're having fun, it's not fun.
Group pressure.
When everyone is laughing, going against it feels huge. Often others are uncomfortable too and are waiting for someone to go first.
📱 Speaking up onlineA laughing emoji is still joining in.

A lot of cruelty happens on screens: pile-ons in comments, screenshots passed around, group chats that exist to talk about someone, or photos of a hangout posted so someone sees what they weren't invited to.

The worst part: online, it's easy to tell yourself a like or a laughing emoji isn't really joining in. To the person on the other end, every one counts.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps: five ways to step in

You don't need a speech. You need one move.

Tap one to see how.

🔀 Distract.

Change the subject, interrupt with a question, or invite the person away. "Hey, want to come with us to the library?"

🧑‍🏫 Delegate.

Get help from an adult or a friend with more influence. Telling an adult is not snitching when someone is getting hurt.

🗣 Direct.

Say something short and clear: "Not cool." "Leave them alone." "That's not funny." Only if it feels safe.

⏳ Delay.

Check in with the person afterward. "I saw what happened. Are you okay?" This matters more than people think.

📸 Document.

Online, screenshot harmful posts to show an adult or report. Then don't share them anywhere else.

🚪 Include.

If someone is on the edge of the group, wait for them, save them a seat, add them to the plan.

Try itHave your line ready before you need it.

Pick or write a few lines you'd actually say. Having them ready makes it easier. Nothing is saved.

Try itThree real moments. What's your move?

Pick a move that could help.

Everyone at your lunch table gets up to go to the bathroom together, and one kid gets left sitting alone. They look down at their phone.

Someone in a group chat posts an unflattering photo of a classmate with a mean caption. People start reacting with laughing emojis.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese usually backfire.
🙈 Assuming someone else will.

Everyone is thinking that. You can be the one who does.

👊 Getting physical.

It can escalate things and get you hurt or in trouble. Get an adult.

🔁 Forwarding it to show it's wrong.

Sharing it spreads it, even with good intentions. Report it instead.

😔 Beating yourself up for past silence.

Everyone has frozen before. What matters is what you do next time.

Get an adult right away if

  • Someone is being physically hurt or threatened.
  • The same person is being targeted over and over.
  • Private or sexual images of someone are being shared.
  • Someone says they want to hurt themselves or others.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. One of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

The Hundred Dresses · Eleanor Estes, 1944

Maddie goes along while her best friend teases Wanda, because she's scared of being next. By the time she's ready to say something, Wanda has moved away. So Maddie decides she'll never stand by and say nothing again.

Horton Hears a Who! · Dr. Seuss, 1954 Throwback

The Whos shout and shout, and nobody hears them until one small kid adds his "Yopp!" One more voice is what tipped it.

🎬 Watch

Wonder · Stephen Chbosky, 2017

When Auggie starts school, most kids keep their distance. Summer just sits down at his lunch table. That's the Include move. Rated PG for thematic elements including bullying and some mild language.

Heads up: Auggie is bullied at school, and there is a fight at a camp.

🎧 Listen

Brave · Sara Bareilles, 2013

Bareilles wrote it for a friend who was scared to say something true. "Say what you wanna say" works for the moment you're about to freeze, too.

🖼️ Look

Each Kindness · Jacqueline Woodson, illustrated by E.B. Lewis, 2012

Look at Lewis's paintings of the new girl, Maya, always a little apart from the others. Chloe never says yes when Maya asks to play, and the book doesn't pretend that's easy to fix.
