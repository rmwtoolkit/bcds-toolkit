Part 1 · Understand it

Feelings and needs

Sound familiar?"Fine" isn't a feeling.
  • Someone asks how you're doing and all you've got is "fine," "good," or "tired."
  • You snapped at your little brother and couldn't explain why.
  • You felt off all day, but couldn't put a name on it.
  • Someone asked "what do you need?" and your mind went blank.
  • Some days just feel great, and you're not sure what made them different.

Most people only have a handful of feeling words. Nobody hands you the rest. This lesson does.

🔔 Feelings are notificationsEvery feeling is a notification from a need.

Every feeling is a notification. It pops up because one of your needs wants your attention.

Lonely is a notification from your need for connection. Cranky is often a notification from your need for sleep or food. Proud is a notification that a need to grow, or to matter, just got met.

A feeling isn't the problem. It's the message.

Your lock screenRed means something's missing. Green means more of this.

Two kinds of notifications

Tap each one.

🔴Need not met

Lonely, worried, frustrated, tired. These say something's missing. Read them before you swipe them away.

🟢Need met

Calm, proud, grateful, curious. These say something's working. They tell you what to do more of.

🔁Same need, both sides

Connection met feels warm and safe. Connection not met feels lonely and hurt. One need, two very different notifications.

🧠 Why bother naming it?Name it to tame it.

In a brain scan study at UCLA, putting a feeling into words calmed the brain's alarm system, the amygdala. Psychiatrist Dan Siegel calls this "name it to tame it."

People who can tell "annoyed" from "hurt" from "embarrassed" also tend to handle stress better. The more specific the word, the easier it is to figure out what would help.

"Bad" tells you almost nothing. "Left out and kind of jealous" tells you exactly where to look.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Reading the notification

What actually helps

Read it before you swipe it away.

Tap one to see how.

🔍 Get specific.

Go one word past "fine" or "stressed." Check the word bank if you're stuck.

🔔 Read the notification.

Before you scroll it away, ask: what is this feeling trying to tell me?

🧭 Look under it.

Ask: what am I missing right now? Sleep? People? A say in this? Some fun?

🗣 Put it together.

"I feel ___ because I need ___." Saying it, even just in your head, makes the next step clearer.

🟢 Notice the good ones too.

When you feel calm or proud, ask what need just got met. That's your recipe.

🧩 Find more than one way.

A need can usually be met lots of ways. If one door is closed, look for another.

These are good guesses, not rules. You're the expert on what's under your own feelings.

Try it"I feel ignored" is a thought in disguise.

Some "feelings" are really thoughts about what someone else did. If you can add "like" or "that" after "I feel," it's usually a thought. Pick one.

"I feel ignored."

"I feel nervous."

"I feel like everyone hates me."

"I feel disappointed."

Thoughts in disguise aren't wrong. They're just one step away from the real feeling, and the real feeling points to the need.

A need can be met more than one wayMost needs have more than one door.
"I need you to text me back."
The need is reassurance and connection. A text is one way. Talking tomorrow, or reaching out to someone else, are others.
"I need to sit at that lunch table."
The need is belonging. That table is one way to get it. A club, a team, or one good friend are others.
"I need everyone to stop bugging me."
The need is space, or peace. Headphones, a walk, or asking for ten minutes can all get you there.
Try itI feel ___ because I need ___.

Think of something small from this week, nothing huge. Fill in the blanks.

👥 In a groupSame story, different feelings, one need.

👥 For advisory or class · about 10 minutes · no materials needed

  1. Read the story on each screen out loud.
  2. Everyone silently picks a feeling word the character might have. Use the word bank if it helps.
  3. A few people share their word. Different words are fine. That's the point.
  4. Vote on the need underneath. Then tap to see one answer.
  5. Flip it: what would the character feel if that need were met?

👥 Talk about itTalk it over.
  • Did people pick different feeling words for the same story? Why might that be?
  • Which was harder to spot, the feeling or the need?
  • Why do you think people often say "fine" instead of naming a real feeling?
  • What's one way a friend could help meet each of these needs?
Tip for the person leading

Keep it on the characters. Nobody should have to share their own feelings out loud. If students disagree about the need, that's a good discussion: a feeling can come from more than one need.

Notifications on your phone, notifications in youThe feed can mute a feeling. It can't meet the need.
📱 Scrolling can mute the notification.
When you're bored, lonely, or tired, the feed is an easy way to not feel it. The need is still there when you look up.
❤️ Likes are a quick hit of "I matter."
Real need, fast and small dose. Texting one friend usually meets it better.
👀 Envy is a notification too.
Jealous of someone's post? Ask what it shows you want. That's information about your needs.
🔕 Before you open the app, name it.
"I'm bored" or "I'm lonely." Then decide if the app is actually what you need.
What doesn't actually help"Fine" on repeat keeps the need waiting.
😐 "Fine" on repeat.

Fine is a way to skip the notification. It doesn't make the need go away.

🗑 Swiping it away.

Ignoring, scrolling, or numbing a feeling means it usually comes back louder.

🙅 Treating the feeling as the problem.

Feelings aren't bad. Getting mad at yourself for feeling sad just adds a second notification.

🎯 Only one way to meet a need.

"It has to be this person, this way" is a trap. Most needs have lots of doors.

📢 Making someone else guess.

People can't read your notifications. Saying "I need ___" gives them a chance to help.

If it gets heavy

  • A red notification won't go away for weeks, or keeps getting louder.
  • You feel numb or hopeless most of the time.
  • A need for safety isn't being met, at home, online, or anywhere else.
  • You have thoughts of not wanting to be here.

Tell a counselor or a trusted adult right away. You can also call or text 988, any time, day or night.
