Don’t forward or react. “Delete this.” Report it. Tell an adult. Check on Maya.

Upstairs

At a party, Ellie from Sam’s team can barely stand. An older guy steers her toward the stairs: “I’ll take care of her.” People look away.

Distract: “Ellie, your sister’s calling, come with me,” and stay with her. Or delegate: her friends, the host.

“Come On”

Two months in. Riley says, “I’m not ready.” Alex goes quiet: “Seriously? Everyone does this. I thought you liked me.” Riley starts to feel guilty.

“I’m not ready” is a full answer. Guilt is how pressure sounds when it’s polite. Alex: “Okay. Thanks for telling me.”

More, if you want it

Why people freeze

In a famous experiment, smoke poured into a room. Students alone reported it 75% of the time. Sitting with two people who calmly kept working, 10% did. Everyone looked fine, so they decided it was fine. Online, the crowd is the group chat, and scrolling past is the new standing by.

Someone usually does step in

In security footage of 219 real fights, at least one person helped in 9 out of 10. Once one person moves, others follow. Being the second person counts.

Lines to have ready
  • “Hey, you okay?” Works almost anywhere.
  • “Not cool. Delete that.” For the group chat.
  • “I’m not ready.” No reason needed.
  • “Okay. Thanks for telling me.” What a respectful answer to no sounds like.
  • “I saw what happened. That was messed up.” Late counts.
Skip these
  • “It’s not my business”If you saw it, you’re part of what happens next, even if all you do is not laugh.
  • “If they didn’t want it out there, they shouldn’t have sent it”The person who forwards it is the one causing the harm.
  • “Guys can’t really be pressured”Anyone can. A guy’s no counts the same.
