Breakups Hurt

It can feel like the end. It isn't.

What's going on

Even if it was short or a crush, it can hurt a lot. You lost someone and a future you imagined, and at school and online, everyone sees. The pain usually fades week by week.

It ends, maybe by text→Everyone at school knows→You keep checking their stories→The bandage rips off again→It feels like forever

Part 1 · Understand it

Why breakups hurt so much

Sound familiar?Even if it was short, it can really hurt.
  • Someone you were "talking to" or "going out with" ended it, maybe by text.
  • A crush you really liked started liking someone else.
  • You changed your mind about someone and don't know how to tell them.
  • Everyone at school seems to know about it, and maybe has an opinion.
  • You keep checking their stories, their Snap score, or who they're with.
  • Friend groups got awkward and you're not sure where you fit now.
  • Nobody knew you two were together, so nobody knows you're hurting.

Whoever you liked, and whatever it looked like, even if it was short or "just" a crush, it can hurt a lot. Your feelings count.

Why it hurts so much

Tap each one.

💔Loss

You lost someone you cared about and a future you imagined. Grief is a normal reaction.

👀It's public

At school and online, everyone sees. You might have to see them every day.

🧩Ripple effects

Shared friends, shared lunch tables, and shared group chats can all shift.

What's going on insideIt hurts because your brain feels it like pain.
Your brain is in withdrawal.
Research suggests romantic rejection activates some of the same brain areas as physical pain. It really can feel like it hurts.
First experiences hit hard.
When something is new, there's nothing to compare it to, so it can feel like it will never get better.
You might feel lots of things at once.
Sad, angry, relieved, embarrassed, lonely. Mixed feelings are completely normal.
It gets better.
The pain usually fades week by week, even if it doesn't feel that way right now.
📱 Breakups onlineEvery check rips the bandage off again.

Checking their stories. Seeing them on Snap Map with someone new. Watching your streak die. Wondering whether to unfollow, archive old photos, or leave the group chat. The phone keeps reopening the wound.

The worst part: every time you check their profile, it's like ripping the bandage off again.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Finding your way through it

What actually helps

You don't have to be over it today.

Tap one to see how.

😢 Let yourself feel it.

Cry, vent, write, listen to sad music for a bit. Feelings move through faster when you let them.

👥 Lean on your people.

Spend time with friends and family who make you feel good. You don't have to talk about the breakup the whole time. If you can't talk to family about it, maybe because of family rules about dating or because you're not out yet, a counselor or another adult you trust can listen.

📵 Take an online break from them.

Mute, archive, or unfollow for a while. You can always change it later. It's about protecting yourself, not making a statement.

🗓 Keep your routine.

School, sleep, activities, eating. Keeping things normal gives your mind something steady.

🛑 Skip the revenge.

Posting about them, sending angry texts, or spreading rumors usually makes you feel worse and can stick around.

🌱 Do something just for you.

Try something new, pick up an old hobby, or spend time on what you love.

Try itSay all of it. You won't hit send.

Write what you wish you could say to them. You won't send it, and nothing here is saved. Getting it out can help.

Try itTough moments. What would actually help?

Pick the most helpful move.

Your "relationship" ended over text, and now everyone in the grade chat is talking about it.

There's usually more than one good answer. The best one is the one you'll actually do.

What doesn't actually helpThese keep the wound open.
🔍 Checking their profiles.

It keeps the wound open.

📲 Drunk-texting or late-night texting.

Feelings run high at night. Wait until morning.

🗣 Talking badly about them.

It can backfire and hurt shared friendships.

🏃 Jumping into something new right away.

It can feel like a fix, but feelings usually catch up.

When to get more help

  • You feel hopeless, or the sadness isn't easing after a few weeks.
  • You're having trouble eating, sleeping, or going to school.
  • Your ex is threatening you, harassing you, or sharing private things.
  • You're thinking about hurting yourself.

A counselor can help. See Get help below.

Other ways in

Sometimes a story or a song says it better. A couple of these you might remember from when you were little.

📖 Read

The Rabbit Listened · Cori Doerrfeld, 2018 Throwback

Something Taylor built gets knocked down. Every animal has advice. Only the rabbit sits close and listens. That's what leaning on your people can look like.

🎬 Watch

Inside Out · Pixar, 2015 Throwback

Joy spends the whole movie trying to keep Sadness away from Riley. It turns out Sadness is what Riley needed. Let yourself feel it. Rated PG.

Sing · Illumination, 2016

Ash the porcupine catches her boyfriend with someone else. She doesn't post about him. She writes her own song and sings it on stage. Rated PG.

🎧 Listen

Stronger (What Doesn't Kill You) · Kelly Clarkson, 2011

A loud, happy song about the day you notice you're doing better without them. Save it for the week the pain starts to fade.

🖼️ Look

Drama · Raina Telgemeier, 2012

Callie runs the stage crew and keeps getting her heart bent by crushes. Her show still goes on, and so does she. A graphic novel.

Is any of this making you feel hopeless, unsafe, or like you might hurt yourself?

Thank you for being honest. Please don't carry this alone. Feeling hopeless or unsafe is a reason to talk to someone today.

  • Call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, any time.
  • The Trevor Project, for LGBTQ+ young people: call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678.
  • Text HOME to 741741, the Crisis Text Line.
  • Tell a counselor, teacher, coach, parent, or another adult you trust today. You can show them this screen.
  • If you might act on thoughts of hurting yourself, call 911, call 988 and ask for a mobile crisis team, or go to an emergency room.

The smallest version counts.

Be as kind to yourself as you would be to a friend going through a breakup. You'd remind them they're worth it. So are you.
