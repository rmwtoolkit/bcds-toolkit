Why starting is so hard

When there’s too much to do, your brain can’t pick where to begin, so it picks nothing.

Too much to do
↓
You get stuck
↓
Screens or your bed
↓
More piles up
↓
“I’m bad at this”
↺ and around again

Make the first step tiny, like writing your name on the worksheet. Once you start, the next step is easier.

When you want to go home

On a field trip, an overnight trip, a sleepover, or just a long school day, you might really want to be home. That makes sense. Home is where you feel safe.

Leaving early makes the next time feel harder. Staying a little longer shows you that you can do it.

Ask to call home right away→Wait ten minutes, then decide
Go to the nurse and stay→Five minutes there, then back to class
Think about home the whole trip→Pick one thing to look forward to

Ms. Graham can help you make a plan for the hard parts.

When you get stuck on one way

Sometimes it feels like it has to be done exactly right, or not at all. That can keep you from starting.

Ask: what’s good enough for today?

Make my poster perfect→Finish the title and one picture
Clean my whole room→Clear my desk
Study everything→Five flashcards
Redo it until it’s perfect→Done is good for today
When grown-ups at home are stressed

Sometimes parents or other grown-ups at home are stressed or busy too.

Making them feel better isn’t your job. You can help with one small thing, like anyone in a family does. If it feels like a lot, tell Ms. Graham or another grown-up you trust.

Grown-ups who can help

Some things you can ask for:

  • “Can you sit with me while I start my homework?”
  • “Can you help me break this into small steps?”
  • “Can we look at my planner together on Sunday?”
  • To a teacher: “I don’t get this part. Can you show me again?”
Your family, your culture

Every family has different jobs for kids. Some kids help take care of younger brothers and sisters, some help cook, some live in two homes. What does your family expect from you? What would help you most right now?

Other ways in
  • Watch: Inside Out (Pixar, 2015, PG). After a move, Riley misses her old home so much she tries to go back.
  • Read: Guts, Raina Telgemeier. A graphic novel about worry that shows up as stomachaches, and what helps.
  • Listen: “Here Comes the Sun,” The Beatles.
  • Look: A bonsai tree. Gardeners shape it with one tiny snip at a time, over years.
  • Make your own: draw your desk the way you want it to look.
