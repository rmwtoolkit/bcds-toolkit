Part 1 · Understand it

What nicotine does to a reward system still being wired

Sound familiar?It started as a few hits. Now it's on a schedule.
  • Someone hits their vape between every class, and gets edgy when a teacher keeps them late.
  • A two-hour exam feels longer than it should because you can't vape in the middle of it.
  • A friend switched to nicotine pouches "because it's not vaping."
  • You or someone you know tried to quit and made it two days.
  • A parent smokes, or vapes to quit smoking, and you've heard very different messages at home and at school.

You've probably heard that vapes have nicotine and that nicotine is addictive. Here's how that actually works in the brain, and why quitting feels the way it does.

7.8%of U.S. high school students vaped in the past 30 days in 2024, down from 10% the year before and the lowest in a decade. Nicotine pouches were the second most used product.National Youth Tobacco Survey, 2024

The loop nicotine plugs into

Ventral tegmental area: the trigger

Nicotine fits receptors that normally respond to acetylcholine. On dopamine neurons in this midbrain area, it makes them fire in bursts within seconds of a hit.

Nucleus accumbens: "do that again"

Dopamine lands here and tags whatever just happened as worth repeating, along with everything around it: the bathroom, the car, the friend, the after-school slump. Those cues start triggering cravings on their own.

Prefrontal cortex: the brakes

Impulse control and attention live here, and they're still maturing until your mid-20s. The Surgeon General's 2016 report concluded that nicotine during adolescence can disrupt the growth of circuits for attention, learning, and susceptibility to addiction.

With regular use, the brain adds more nicotine receptors to cope. Now it needs nicotine just to feel normal. That's dependence.

The stress trapIt relieves stress it created.
PARROTT, 1999

Psychologist Andy Parrott reviewed the research on smokers and stress. His conclusion: nicotine does not lower stress overall. Dependent users feel tense and irritable as nicotine wears off, and the next dose brings them back to roughly where non-users already are. The relief is real. The stress it relieves is mostly withdrawal.

The same studies found that people who quit report feeling less stressed over time, not more, once withdrawal passes.

The researchFaster, and more tangled, than people expect.
Dependence can start within weeks.
In a study of 681 seventh graders in central Massachusetts (DiFranza, 2000), 22% of those who had started smoking occasionally reported a symptom of dependence within four weeks of starting to smoke monthly. Many had symptoms before they ever smoked daily. That study was about cigarettes; the same nicotine is in vapes, often at higher doses.
For teens, vaping is linked to smoking later.
The National Academies of Sciences (2018) found substantial evidence that e-cigarette use increases the risk that young people will try regular cigarettes.
The honest tradeoff.
The same report found conclusive evidence that e-cigarette aerosol has fewer and lower levels of most toxic chemicals than cigarette smoke. That matters for an adult smoker who switches completely. For someone who doesn't smoke, a vape doesn't reduce harm. It adds nicotine dependence.
THC carts carry their own risks.
In 2019 and 2020, the CDC counted 2,807 people hospitalized and 68 deaths from a vaping lung injury called EVALI. Most cases were tied to vitamin E acetate in THC carts bought from friends, dealers, or online.
Why vapes hit so hardNicotine salts, big batteries, no stopping point.

Many vapes use nicotine salts, a form of nicotine that's smoother to inhale at high concentrations, so a high dose doesn't feel harsh. Disposables can last thousands of puffs, and there's no natural end point like finishing a cigarette.

Massachusetts banned flavored vapes in 2019, the first state to do so, and raised the buying age to 21 in 2018. Flavored disposables still turn up through illegal imports and online sellers, often with no reliable label.

Part 2 · Practice the skills

Breaking the loop

What actually helps

Quitting is a skill. It has steps.

GRAHAM AND COLLEAGUES, JAMA, 2024

In a randomized trial with 1,503 teens aged 13 to 17 who vaped, 37.8% of those using the free This is Quitting text program had stopped vaping seven months later, compared with 28.0% of those who did not get it. The program was built and studied by Truth Initiative.

Tap each one.

1 Map your loop.

For a few days, notice every craving: where you were, who you were with, what you felt. Most people find three or four cues that drive most of it. Those are what you plan around.

2 Know the timeline.

Withdrawal is usually worst in the first few days: irritable, restless, foggy, hungry, bad sleep. It fades over a few weeks. A single craving often peaks and passes within minutes if you ride it out.

3 Use the free program.

Text DITCHVAPE to 88709 for This is Quitting. It's free, anonymous, and built for people your age.

4 Bring in a professional.

A doctor, nurse, or counselor can help you plan, and can talk with you about whether nicotine replacement like patches or gum makes sense for you. It's about your health, not getting in trouble.

5 Swap the cue along with the vape.

If the cue is the walk between classes, change the route or the company. If it's stress, pick a replacement that takes a minute: water, a walk, music, a slow exhale longer than the inhale.

6 Have your line ready.

"I'm quitting." "I'm good." Short is enough. Friends who care will back you up.

What doesn't helpThese usually keep the loop going.
MYTH I can stop anytime.

Most people who end up dependent thought the same. The easiest way to find out is to try a week without it.

MYTH Switching to a lower-nicotine vape is quitting.

It can be a step down, but it keeps the cue and the habit alive. Pair it with a real plan or it tends to drift back up.

MYTH Going cold turkey alone, in secret.

Some people manage it, but support helps. In the 2024 trial, a free text program alone raised quit rates by about 10 points.

MYTH One slip means I failed.

Most people who quit for good had slips first. A slip is information about a cue you missed.

Get help right away if

  • You or a friend has trouble breathing, chest pain, or a cough that won't go away after vaping.
  • Someone gets very sick, confused, or passes out after using a vape, especially one that might contain THC.
  • A young child swallows vape liquid. Nicotine liquid is poisonous.

For an emergency, call 911. Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. See Get help below.
