Guess first

Who gets liked more after a first chat: the one with great stories, or the one who asks questions?

AnswerCurious beats clever. Strangers who asked more questions were liked more, and follow-ups did the most work: “Wait, what happened?”

Go one layer down

Then trade: they share, you share.

Facts, then opinions, then stories. You don’t need a great opener. You need a good second question.

Guess first

How often does a conversation end when both people want it to?

AnswerAbout 2% of the time. So end it cleanly and leave a thread: “Gotta go. Tell me how the audition goes.”

Mastroianni et al., PNAS, 2021

Sound familiar?

What’s your move?

One-word answers

You ask a classmate about the game. “Fine.” You ask what’s next. “Dunno.” They’re looking at their phone.

One easier pass (an either-or question, or something right in front of you), then a friendly exit: “Cool, see you in chem.” It’s rarely about you.

The table

You know one person at a lunch table. The rest are deep into a show you’ve never seen.

Sit near your one person, listen for the topic, then join on it with a question: “Wait, is that the one with the twins?”

The text after

You had a good talk with someone at practice. You have their number. Now what?

Use the thread: “How’d the audition go?” or a meme about what you talked about. Both beat “hey.”

More, if you want it

Shift it, or follow it?

“I had a terrible game.” Shifting: “Mine was worse.” Following: “What happened?” Following keeps the other person talking and makes them like you more.

If you get one-word answers

Try one easier pass, like an either-or question or something right in front of you. Then a friendly exit: “Cool, see you in chem.” Not every chat takes off.
